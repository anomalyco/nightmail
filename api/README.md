# Nightmail setup and operations

One Hono/TypeScript API for Node/Docker and Cloudflare Workers. The dashboard and generated SDK use that same public API; MCP discovers its OpenAPI contract. PostgreSQL is the source of truth: queued mail rows are the dispatch queue, a Postgres outbox drives campaign orchestration and webhooks, and private R2/S3 stores attachments. On Cloudflare a Durable Object per account/region hosts the long-lived dispatcher and a queue carries verified provider feedback; no Redis, D1, passwords, teams, or production admin bypass.

## Large campaigns

For Hosted Ohio receiving bootstrap, readiness, SNS dead-letter replay, quota increases and the exact production rollout checks, see [Receiving operations](../docs/receiving-operations.md). See [receiving measurements](../docs/receiving-measurements.md) for the SQL and storage budgets.

Send and schedule need only the current `revision`. The request freezes the audience and content into one snapshot, reserves the recipients and moves the campaign to `preparing`; the `202` response means that background workflow was accepted, not that validation or SES delivery has finished. `GET /v1/campaigns/{id}/audience` counts the saved audience (matched, eligible, suppressed, unsubscribed) before a send is confirmed; it is a live read and freezes nothing. In MCP Code Mode, `previewCampaignAudience` exposes the same count; `sendCampaign` and `scheduleCampaign` take the current revision.

**Postal address (CAN-SPAM).** US law requires the sender's postal address in every commercial email. Each workspace keeps one: `postalAddress` on `GET`/`PATCH /v1/settings/workspace` (Settings → General in the dashboard), plain text of at most 300 characters on up to 6 lines, no markup, each line trimmed and empty ones dropped; owners and admins (and keys with `manage`) change it, and every change is audited. Every campaign email shows it under the Unsubscribe link in the same style, in the HTML and the text part, and so do the campaign preview, its PNG and campaign tests. Send and schedule refuse with `422 POSTAL_ADDRESS_REQUIRED` (`field: "postalAddress"`) while it is unset, after the launch's admission check and before the audience is captured; the dashboard asks for it in place when Send or Schedule is pressed, then carries on. The launch freezes the address in its review, so every copy, retry and rebuilt email of that launch shows the same one and a later change reaches only later launches. A campaign test needs none and shows the footer as it will look, with a placeholder link and no `List-Unsubscribe`. A campaign launched before postal addresses existed froze none: before its first recipient is released it takes the workspace's, stamped into its review, or, with none set, pauses with `POSTAL_ADDRESS_REQUIRED` until one is added and it is resumed; one that had already released recipients finishes as it started. Marketing single sends (`kind: "marketing"`) get the address under their Unsubscribe link when the workspace has one but are not refused without one: their caller writes the whole message, and refusing them would break integrations that work today. Transactional mail gets no footer. Migration 393 adds the columns (nullable, `NOT VALID` length checks: no rewrite or scan).

**Required notices.** A campaign with `requiredNotice: true` is a message every recipient must get whatever their marketing choice (a change to terms, prices or sales tax), never a promotion. It goes to everyone in its audience who is not suppressed: people who unsubscribed from marketing or never subscribed get it, addresses suppressed for a hard bounce or a complaint and deleted contacts never do, at capture and again at dispatch. It has no Unsubscribe link, no `List-Unsubscribe` headers and no unsubscribe token; its footer is the postal address alone, which it still needs. It changes nobody's consent. Everything else is a campaign's: the marketing configuration set, the transactional reserve, daily limits, the customer share, marketing holds, the list probe, automatic pauses and the content screen. Send and schedule need `confirmRequiredNotice: true` (`422 REQUIRED_NOTICE_UNCONFIRMED`), the sender's confirmation that it has no promotion, recorded as a `campaign.required_notice_confirmed` audit event (who and when, in Activity). A workspace makes or sends one only once an operator turned them on (`403 REQUIRED_NOTICES_OFF`; `requiredNotices` on `GET /v1/settings/workspace`): from `api/`, `node --import tsx scripts/set-required-notices.ts "$WID" on|off --reason "why"`, Hosted or self-hosted, audited in `hosted_operator_audit`. Migration 402 adds `workspaces.required_notices` (off) and turns it on for exempt (first-party) workspaces. Turned off, a launched notice pauses before its next release (`REQUIRED_NOTICES_OFF`: a scheduled one at its time, a paced one at its next window; mail already queued, a few seconds of the send rate, still goes, as with any pause), and resume and reschedule refuse it until they are on again; then a resume continues where it stopped. Resumed after a list-probe quarantine, a notice goes on to everyone captured for it: unlike a marketing campaign it is never restricted to contacts the workspace delivered to before.

A campaign has one `state`, written only by `src/states.ts`: `draft` → `preparing` (every message validated in the background; `delivery.prepared` is the cursor) → `scheduled` (waiting for `scheduledAt`) or `sending` (recipients released into the dispatch queue; `delivery.expanded`) → `done`, set by the dispatcher once the queue drains for the campaigns it moved rows for (one statement per draining batch, campaign rows only; the minute sweep finishes any it missed). `paused` is entered by the user or the health check and resumes to wherever the cursors say. `failed` keeps `delivery.error`: if nothing was released yet it can be edited (back to `draft`) or sent again from a fresh snapshot; once recipients were released it can only be resumed (continuing from `delivery.expanded` on the same snapshot, so nobody receives the message twice) or canceled, and it keeps its reservation until then. `canceled` is terminal. One job row per campaign (`campaign.run:<id>`) does all of the work and waits in place for the schedule, the dispatch buffer, pacing or the SES quota, so a campaign can never be left `sending` with nothing queued or `preparing` after its job exhausted its retries: the last retry or a definitive error leaves `failed`.

Only referenced personalization properties are copied into immutable recipient rows; the rendered base email is shared. Email rows are materialized progressively rather than all existing immediately; future schedules reserve capacity without filling the active dispatch buffer. `delivery` reports the cursors, pacing, pause and hold reasons; its `canceled` count is unmaterialized cancellations, separate from canceled email rows. Final personalized audit snapshots are saved at dispatch. Never manually replay an uncertain SES send.

| Bound | Current implementation |
| --- | --- |
| Matching contacts per campaign | 2,000,000, before consent/suppression filtering |
| Concurrent campaign runs | Bounded by worker/runner job concurrency; other campaigns remain durable queue work |
| Referenced personalization snapshot | 1 GiB per campaign, 1 MiB per recipient |
| Preparation/release chunk | Up to 20,000 / 2,000 recipients respectively; preparation is also bounded to 4 MiB and ten seconds per step |
| Active campaign dispatch buffer | Ten seconds of the cached regional send rate per campaign (400 floor, 20,000 cap) and twenty seconds per workspace (1,000 floor, 50,000 cap). On MX, each shared workspace plans against 25% of the pool rate; BYO uses its full account rate. The daily quota and dispatcher gate remain account-wide. |
| Outstanding campaign intents | 2,000,000 per originating credential; 4,000,000 per workspace |
| Orchestration jobs per process/invocation | `JOB_CONCURRENCY`: default 2, maximum 8 for Node and 6 for Cloudflare (campaign runs, webhooks, maintenance; never mail) |
| Dispatcher shards | Derived automatically from the SES account's `MaxSendRate` (`ceil(MaxSendRate / 25)`, capped at 256: 8 shards at 200/s, 40 at 1,000/s), with `DISPATCH_SHARDS` as the cold-start fallback before the first quota sample (1 for BYO before first sample). Each shard paces 1/N of the regional quota with up to six provider calls in flight and hands off cleanly when AWS raises `MaxSendRate`. Sending up to six emails wakes one shard per target region; larger bursts wake all shards. The Node runner dispatches in-process. |

These are application bounds, not a guaranteed SES send rate. Mail is dispatched by long-lived batch dispatchers (`src/dispatcher.ts`): one statement leases up to twenty due rows, one transaction elects the batch (origin authorization, consent locks, campaign cancellation, unsubscribe tokens, attempt events), provider calls run six-wide behind an in-memory pacing gate, and one statement records every outcome. `sending_region_limits` is the shared one-minute quota cache; only a shard that finds it stale calls `GetAccount`. No SES request holds a database transaction, and a row is sent at most once because the claim flips it `queued → attempting` under its dispatch version. Direct sends retain their separate pending limits. Node runners hold one in-process gate each, so run one runner per installation or split the quota explicitly.

Apply migrations through `019_batch_dispatcher.sql` before deploying this backend; pre-migration `email.dispatch` job rows complete as no-ops once the dispatcher owns queued rows. For Docker, stop old runners and upgrade API/runner images together; the new runner continues a previous release's `campaign.prepare/expand/finish` rows as runs of the same campaign, but old runners do not recognize `campaign.run`. Do not roll back to an older backend while campaigns are active.

## Configuration

**MX regional sending (operator rollout):** Apply migrations through 071, then deploy the compatible API, runners and dispatchers with `NIGHTMAIL_REGIONAL_SENDING=false` (the default). The backfill copies the current connection, domain ID and region, not retained mail. A metadata lock timeout means retry the migration, not disable the timeout. Do not enable the flag until old instances have drained and the regional IAM/SES canaries pass. The flag applies only to `MULTI_TENANT=true`: From's closest claimed domain selects the sending region, each batch item resolves independently, and a conflicting explicit region rejects the send. It does not publish a new stack, provision Virginia, move a domain, or change receiving DNS. Disable the flag to stop new regional admissions; retain the schema and compatible dispatchers so already-pinned work and late feedback keep their original bindings. Workspaces with managed bindings cannot fall back to singleton sending.

Disabling a *region* retires its binding generation. The minute recovery sweep cancels up to 1,000 still-queued envelopes per pass with `SENDER_BINDING_CHANGED`, including scheduled mail. It leaves attempts alone; resuming a region never reauthorizes old work. Draft editing, history filters and campaign controls remain available.

**Hosted trusted sending (operator only, migration 081):** `NIGHTMAIL_TRUSTED_POOL=false` by default. Requires both `MULTI_TENANT=true` and `NIGHTMAIL_REGIONAL_SENDING=true`. Ohio remains the default and the only receiving pool. Shadow scores do not route traffic. From `api/`, `node --import tsx scripts/set-domain-pool.ts "$WID" DOMAIN trusted --reason "why"` records initial placement and queues `hosted.trusted`. `probation` selects Ohio. The trusted tier also keeps the domain's mail out of the probation content rules, which otherwise end by themselves on good standing. `"$WID" --prepare-trusted --reason "why"` prepares just the workspace tenant without selecting any domain. Set the same three flags in the script environment. Check `jobs.last_error`, the regional states and the operator audit before enrollment. A set/topic or tenant with ambiguous ownership requires manual review, not automatic repair. Do not use a tier change to move an existing binding or bypass a hold. A live move needs separate target-identity staging, DNS consent, canary and drain.

**Dedicated Hosted accounts:** Use the [three offline-rendered IAM policies](../infra/aws-shared/README.md) in the matching account. Prepare Ohio receiving first. Verify signed simulator delivery and tenant isolation in each enabled pool before admitting customer traffic. There is no identity-adoption manifest or shared-account release checker for these accounts.

**Platform domain (operator only, migration 107):** `PUBLIC_URL`'s host and its subdomains are refused to every workspace except `NIGHTMAIL_PLATFORM_DOMAIN_WORKSPACE` (Hosted with regional sending only, unset by default), and that workspace claims nothing there until an operator has adopted the root. Even then it never claims, receives on or sends from the platform's own hosts: `dev.`, `e2e.`/`e2e-*.`, `in.`, `domainconnect.`, `imap.`, `smtp.`, `tracker.` and everything below them (`reservedPlatformName` in `src/domain-claims.ts`; add new platform hosts there before they go live). A release fixture that holds its own `e2e*` claim keeps sending from it. The sender policy lives in `regional_sender`, which admission, campaign reviews, mailbox replies and dispatch all read with the running configuration: removing or changing the variable stops the workspace sending as the platform domain at once, including mail already queued. Receiving then ends with the usual 72-hour proof grace; delete the domain to stop it at once.

The root's SES identity was made by hand and has no claim tag, so only an operator adopts it. From `api/`, with a short-lived DB role and a signed-in operator role in the platform account, run `node --import tsx scripts/adopt-platform-domain.ts "$WID"` (dry run). Review its DKIM tokens, MAIL FROM and default set, then run it again with `--apply --fingerprint <the dry run's fingerprint> --reason "why"`; a different live identity is refused, and so is one with sending authorization policies. It pins that fingerprint, associates the identity with the workspace's Ohio tenant (its only AWS write), records `platform-domain-intent` and `platform-domain-adopted` in `hosted_operator_audit`, and chooses the root as the receiving host with a `store` catch-all: unknown recipients are kept as unrouted mail and never become addresses. The validation mailboxes a certificate authority may use (`admin@`, `administrator@`, `hostmaster@`, `postmaster@`, `webmaster@`, `root@`) are never created automatically under the platform domain, even with a `create_address` catch-all; only an inbox created on purpose receives them. SES's own DKIM check stands in for the `_nightmail` TXT. Nightmail never creates, changes or deletes that identity, and the runtime policy can't; deleting the domain only releases the claim. The fingerprint is checked daily and on **Check now**: a changed identity then stops sending and receiving together, and only another dry run and `--apply` restore it. To undo an adoption, including an aborted one, run the script with `--revoke` (dry run), then `--revoke --apply --reason "why"`: it releases the claim, removes the identity from the workspace's tenant and records `platform-domain-revoked`. It works even after the variable was changed.

**Hosted bulk DMARC (opt-in):** `NIGHTMAIL_REQUIRE_DMARC_FOR_BULK=false` by default. Enable only after checking senders' domain readiness and allowing time for DNS repairs. When enabled, admitting a marketing email or launching/scheduling a campaign requires a checked exact From domain with an effective DMARC policy (including inherited `sp` or `p`) and aligned DKIM or custom MAIL FROM. Unknown, stale or invalid observations fail with `BULK_DMARC_REQUIRED`; transactional mail and BYO are unaffected. This is an admission check, not a recall of already-queued mail. MX verification and periodic DNS upkeep populate the cached observation; **Check now** requests a fresh check. A recommended `p=none` TXT is for an empty configuration only. Nightmail does not ingest aggregate reports; add `rua` only if you operate a reporting destination. Inventory legitimate senders before tightening the policy.

**Upgrading from the live/test split:** release `052_prepare_drop_environment.sql` with the backend that stops using environment columns. Migration 052 permanently discards test data and test-only OAuth approvals, but keeps the columns, defaults and conflict targets needed by the previous single-mode backend (`2f350fa`). Release `053_drop_environment.sql` separately, only after all API instances, runners and dispatchers use the new backend and old invocations have drained. After 053, do not roll back to a backend that names those columns. Fresh installations can apply both migrations together.

Use Node.js **24** and Docker Compose for the local quickstart. All commands below run from `api/` unless stated otherwise.

```sh
bun install
cp .env.example .env
```

Fill `.env` locally; never commit it or paste credentials into the dashboard. Generate each secret independently with:

```sh
node -e 'console.log(require("node:crypto").randomBytes(32).toString("hex"))'
```

## Dashboard sign-in

Register a confidential web client with your OIDC issuer. Register `${PUBLIC_URL}/api/auth/callback/account` as its exact callback and `${PUBLIC_URL}/` as its post-logout URI. Upload the public P-256 JWK and select `private_key_jwt` (ES256). Supply the matching private JWK through your server secret manager. Use the same issuer and client settings for the API and job runner. A self-hosted issuer must support discovery, PKCE S256, signed ID tokens and the `openid email profile` scopes. Nightmail uses PAR when advertised.

Identity is `(issuer, subject)`; the account's email address is display and contact data only, because an issuer can assert a verified address that the sign-in method never vouched for. The account service gives an account one subject whichever of its linked methods signs in, so a person who uses GitHub one day and Google the next stays one Nightmail user; an account the service keeps separate is a separate user. A new subject is admitted only by: the sign-in's provider identity (hosted staff, `AUTH_STAFF_GOOGLE_DOMAINS`, Google only), a pending invitation link it followed (the invitation also has to name its verified address, and acceptance binds the membership to the subject), a place under the hosted sign-up limit (below), `AUTH_ALLOWED_SUBJECTS` (self-hosted), or an operator binding the subject to a user. Nightmail records every refused, verified sign-in by subject (`nightmail_signin_attempts`, kept 30 days); list them with `node --import tsx scripts/list-signin-attempts.ts`, confirm with the person out of band, then add the subject to `AUTH_ALLOWED_SUBJECTS` or link it to an existing user (`INSERT INTO nightmail_oidc_identities(issuer,subject,user_id)`). On a hosted installation, a refused sign-in with a verified address and no invitation also joins the waitlist (`nightmail_waitlist`, one row per address; it leaves when a sign-in with that address is admitted, or a year after its last attempt). The refusal screen says we'll email that address when Nightmail opens; operators list, search and export the waitlist at **Operator → Waitlist** (`GET /v1/operator/waitlist`). The waitlist admits nobody by itself. **The sign-up limit** (`src/signups.ts`, migration 404) is how many accounts may sign up on their own; it starts at 0, which changes nothing. An operator sets it at **Operator → Sign-ups** (`PUT /v1/operator/signups`, audited in `hosted_operator_audit` as workspace `platform`). A place is taken by an account that signed up (`nightmail_signups`, one row per account, written in the transaction that creates it, so it counts once) or held for a waiting address. A raise holds the places it adds for the oldest waiting addresses (first attempt first) and emails them; places left over go to whoever signs in next. An open raise (`raise: "open"`, "Open to anyone" in the dialog) holds none and emails nobody: its places go to whoever signs in next, waiting or not. Places already open stay open after either kind. Lowering it removes nobody and keeps held places. A new subject that isn't staff, follows no invitation and signs in with a mailbox it proves (`provenMailbox`: an emailed link or code, or a Google-hosted address; never GitHub or a password) takes the place held for that address, else an open one, under a lock on the limit, and its workspace is created as a staff sign-up's is; at the limit it joins the waitlist as before. Staff, invitees and bound subjects take no place. After a change of the limit commits, `placesOpened` emails every held address not yet told that it can sign in (`waitlistAdmitted`, one email per address ever), so a failed or slow email never undoes the change; the answer counts who was emailed and who wasn't, and saving the limit again emails anyone a failure missed. The Waitlist tab shows when each address was let in and whether its email went. A GitHub sign-in takes a place (held for its address, else open) with the GitHub account's verified primary address (`githubVerifiedAddress`: the provider identity claim's primary, verified GitHub address, never the account's asserted one), one place per address. So does any sign-in that follows a **sign-in link** emailed to an address (migration 466). A sign-in that shows nothing of its address (GitHub without a verified primary email, a password) is refused; when a place is held for its address (`?place=held`) or open (`?place=ready`), the refusal screen offers a sign-in link to it in one click, never the account service's email sign-in (which a GitHub- or Google-made account can't use). Anyone refused can ask for one to the address they joined with (`POST /api/auth/sign-in/link`, the same answer whether or not one is sent; it goes only to a waiting address with a place held for it or while one is open, at most three a day to one address). The link carries a row ID and its HMAC under a key derived from the root secret, works once and for 30 minutes, and proves that mailbox for that place only: the session's proven mailbox, invitations and email support are unchanged (`?link=expired` when it no longer works). Nobody is linked to an existing user by address; the migration only copies existing `(issuer, subject)` links. On a hosted installation a new subject also needs a verified address, whatever the method: without one it is refused before staff or an invitation is considered. A missing or unverified address never blocks an admitted subject's sessions, MCP access or queued sends. What is bound to an address (an invitation, an emailed support request) needs a sign-in that proves its mailbox now: on a hosted installation, an emailed link or code (the account service sends it only to the account's verified address, whoever hosts that mailbox), or a Google sign-in with that address hosted by Google (Workspace or Gmail). A GitHub or password sign-in can't redeem one, because it shows only that an address was verified once.

**The sign-ups list** (hosted, `src/signups.ts`). With `NIGHTMAIL_SIGNUPS_LIST_ID` set to a list in the platform workspace, everyone who joins the waitlist or signs up on their own is a contact of that list, subscribed from when they first asked, with the consent source `Nightmail waitlist` (`Nightmail sign-up` for an account that never waited). Its properties, to segment by: `status` (`waiting`, `let_in`, `signed_up`, or `admitted` for one an invitation or staff let in after it joined, whose consent stays as the waitlist left it), `joined_at`, `let_in_at` and `signed_up_at` (ISO 8601 in UTC), `attempts`, `email_domain` and `mailbox` (`free` or `company`); its name is the one the sign-in gave. Nothing on the sign-in or raise path writes for it: a waitlist row is due when it changed since it was last written (`list_synced_at`, migration 459), a sign-up until it has been, and every minute a `signups.sync` job writes what is due through the audience code (`upsertListContact` in `src/audience.ts`): the API's checks and limits, an opt-out stays an opt-out, suppression is never touched and a deleted contact stays deleted. A sign-up's contact comes from what its admission recorded (the mailbox it proved and the waitlist row it consumed), never the account's later profile. Invitation and staff admissions are never sign-up consent: an hourly `signups.reconcile` only marks `admitted` a contact that already joined from the waitlist. Every row starts due, so the first runs after the list is set write the whole waitlist; `node --import tsx scripts/sync-signups-list.ts` writes it all again (after pointing the variable at another list).

Nightmail stores a hash of the browser's random session token and checks the session expiry and workspace membership in PostgreSQL. Signing out revokes the Nightmail session.

On sign-in Nightmail refreshes the address and verification flag from the signed ID token or subject-checked UserInfo response. An address change invalidates prior sessions. A missing or unverified address cannot accept invitations. When moving a test installation from pairwise to public subjects, review the user's identity and current mailbox first. With sign-ins fenced, remove the obsolete `nightmail_oidc_identities` binding and approve the user's exact address in `bane_legacy_claims` for the expected issuer. The next verified sign-in binds the public subject to the original Nightmail user ID.

## Docker quickstart

The image bundles the dashboard with the API on one origin, **http://127.0.0.1:8793**. The API and background worker are separate services using the same image and database. Stop any host API using port 8793 first.

1. Start the local infrastructure after completing `.env`:

   ```sh
   docker compose -p bane-api-local up -d postgres storage
   ```

   Postgres is exposed on `127.0.0.1:55432`; MinIO on `127.0.0.1:59000`. Wait until both services are ready before provisioning the bucket.

2. Create the private local bucket **once on a fresh installation**:

   ```sh
   node --env-file=.env --input-type=module - <<'JS'
   import { S3Client, CreateBucketCommand } from '@aws-sdk/client-s3';
   const e = process.env;
   const client = new S3Client({
     endpoint: e.S3_ENDPOINT, region: e.S3_REGION, forcePathStyle: true,
     credentials: { accessKeyId: e.S3_ACCESS_KEY_ID, secretAccessKey: e.S3_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY }
   });
   await client.send(new CreateBucketCommand({ Bucket: e.S3_BUCKET }));
   JS
   ```

   Do not recreate an existing bucket or make it public. Managed storage can instead be provisioned through its provider.

3. Build and apply migrations to the intended database:

   ```sh
   docker compose -p bane-api-local build api
   docker compose -p bane-api-local run --rm api node dist/migrate.js
   ```

   Migrations are explicit, serialized, and checksum-checked. HTTP startup does not run them automatically.

4. Start both processes:

   ```sh
   docker compose -p bane-api-local up -d api worker
   ```

   Open `http://127.0.0.1:8793` and sign in using your approved account. The image runs as the unprivileged `node` user and excludes local secrets/test files.

5. Create a scoped API key for SDK/MCP calls. Check `GET /v1/me`: `simulated: true` means this deployment will not send through SES. Simulation still writes local data and delivers configured webhooks.

For managed infrastructure, run the same image with your actual database/private storage settings rather than the local Compose overrides. API command: `node dist/server.js`; background worker: `node dist/runner.js`. Multiple workers use Postgres row locks and leases to claim jobs safely. Put public deployments behind HTTPS, with `PUBLIC_URL` set to the browser-visible origin.

Use the same local Postgres/MinIO and bucket as above, but stop Docker's `api`/`worker` before running host processes. Keep Node.js 24 active in each terminal.

## Database and storage

Use ordinary PostgreSQL, including managed providers such as PlanetScale Postgres. For remote Node/migration connections, require `sslmode=verify-full` and a verifiable certificate; do not disable certificate validation. Only localhost and the local Compose `postgres` hostname may omit TLS. Use your provider's actual **Postgres** connection URL, with URL-encoded credentials. For PlanetScale pooled URLs, follow its current pooler restrictions and use a supported direct connection for migrations if the pooler cannot support migration locking/session behavior. Do not substitute a MySQL connection string. See [PlanetScale Postgres connections](https://planetscale.com/docs/postgres/connecting).

Attachments belong in a **private** S3/R2 bucket. Set the bucket, region, endpoint, and storage credentials for your provider; never put them in client-side environment variables. Local MinIO root credentials are development-only—not a recommended production access policy. Keep database and object-storage backups together. Object keys belong to the installation's data namespace; do not rename/move them casually during upgrades.

On upgrade, dashboard users sign in again and theme/workspace preferences reset once. Existing API keys, scoped OAuth grants, signed webhook secrets, and published domain-ownership proofs continue to work. New proof records use `_nightmail`; do not replace an existing `_bane` or `_opensend` record just to rename it.

Hosted received mail can use private Cloudflare R2 storage: `MAIL` is distinct from sending uploads. `NIGHTMAIL_MAIL_STORE=r2` switches new shared-pool writes (and enables agent inboxes); unset it to resume S3 writes. Agent inbox mail stays in R2 while `MAIL` is bound. Keep the binding for existing downloads. SES staging (3 days) and quarantine (30 days) remain in S3. Non-inbox raw mail still expires after 90 days; extracted attachments do not expire. BYO and self-hosted mail stays in its configured S3 bucket.

## Cloudflare

Every `NIGHTMAIL_*` setting is also read under its earlier names, `BANE_*` and then `MX_*` (`PREVIOUS_NIGHTMAIL_ROOT_SECRET` also as `PREVIOUS_BANE_ROOT_SECRET`); the `NIGHTMAIL_*` name wins. This repository's Worker configs set the `NIGHTMAIL_*` names; its root secret is still deployed as `BANE_ROOT_SECRET`. Set `NIGHTMAIL_*` names for new deployments.

`wrangler.prod.jsonc` contains the production bindings for **https://nightmail.dev** on the separate `nightmail-production` Worker. `wrangler.dev.jsonc` targets the `nightmail-dev` Worker. To self-host in another account, replace `account_id`, the custom-domain route and `PUBLIC_URL`, the Hyperdrive ID, and the R2/Queue names with your own resources; create the queues (`wrangler queues create nightmail-work`, `nightmail-webhooks`, `nightmail-feedback-v2`, `nightmail-feedback-v2-dlq` and `nightmail-feedback-dlq`) before the first deploy. On a fresh installation drop only `LEGACY_FEEDBACK_QUEUE_NAME`, `LEGACY_FEEDBACK_CUTOVER` and the original legacy consumer. Keep both archive-only DLQ consumers and their name/binding settings: each is the other's failure destination. The `DispatcherShard` and `CampaignRoom` Durable Object migrations are applied by `wrangler deploy`; `DISPATCH_SHARDS` sets how many shards share each region's quota. `CAMPAIGN_ROOMS` (one `CampaignRoom` per campaign) shows who else has a campaign open in the dashboard; without the binding, as on the Node server, the dashboard shows no presence. Do not reuse production bindings for staging or preview builds. The dashboard build is served from `app/dist` as Workers assets on the API's origin.

Use a read/write database role for Hyperdrive and a separate schema-owner credential for direct Node migrations. Configure Hyperdrive origin TLS as `verify-full`; Cloudflare requires an uploaded CA certificate ID for that mode. The deployed connection uses the validated ISRG Root X1 trust anchor. Node migration connections accept PlanetScale's `sslrootcert=system` URI parameter without treating it as a filesystem path, while retaining certificate and hostname verification.

**Disable Hyperdrive query caching** with `--caching-disabled`; a shorter TTL is not equivalent. Authentication, consent, idempotency, and job state need fresh reads. Hyperdrive still provides pooling. See [query caching](https://developers.cloudflare.com/hyperdrive/concepts/query-caching/).

Supply `NIGHTMAIL_ROOT_SECRET` (or the earlier `BANE_ROOT_SECRET`) and `ACCOUNT_CLIENT_PRIVATE_KEY` using Worker secrets; configure your real `ACCOUNT_CLIENT_ID`, allowlists, and HTTPS `PUBLIC_URL` for this deployment. This deployment also stores `ACCOUNT_CLIENT_ID` and the allowlists as Worker secrets rather than committing those installation values. For AWS discovery, explicit provisioning, or live SES sending, also supply `AWS_ACCESS_KEY_ID`, `AWS_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY`, and optional `AWS_SESSION_TOKEN` securely. Use interactive `wrangler secret put --config wrangler.prod.jsonc` (or the dev config), not secrets in config or command arguments. Pasted temporary credentials are not automatically renewed.

Build `app/` with `bun install` and `bun run build` before `bun run cf:check` in `api/`. `cf:check` is a deployment **dry run**, not a deployment or remote-resource check. For local Workers, keep an ignored `api/.dev.vars` containing only the required Worker settings and secrets, including both OIDC credentials, allowlists, `NIGHTMAIL_ROOT_SECRET`, and the local `PUBLIC_URL`. This avoids loading unrelated Docker/storage credentials from `.env`. Securely set `CLOUDFLARE_HYPERDRIVE_LOCAL_CONNECTION_STRING_HYPERDRIVE`, then use `bun run cf:dev -- --port 8794 --local-upstream 127.0.0.1:8794 --upstream-protocol http` and a matching `PUBLIC_URL`/OIDC callback. The explicit local upstream prevents Wrangler from rewriting same-origin authorization headers to the production custom domain. Local Hyperdrive cannot certify production pooling/caching; see [local development](https://developers.cloudflare.com/hyperdrive/configuration/local-development/).

HTTP, Queue, and scheduled handlers share the outbox. Queue messages are wakeups; committed jobs survive a lost wakeup. Workers consume one wake signal per queue invocation and stop claiming new jobs after a two-second turn, while allowing every in-flight job to finish. Database claims lease only as many jobs as there are free local lanes; lanes refill individually as jobs finish, without waiting for the slowest job in a group. At most one feedback-first reservation is active per invocation, with feedback-only fallback when no send work exists. Invocation-local rotation provides job-class fairness without writing a shared scheduler row on every claim. The `job_schedule` row remains for admission locking and wake coalescing. A campaign launch asks for an uncoalesced wake so its run starts at once. Cron recovers due work and runs retention: every table hourly, and the sent-side tables every 15 minutes. PostgreSQL transactions/leases, not wake messages or process-local state, control dispatch.

Apply migration `018_wake_coalescing.sql` before deploying this Worker; singleton wake coalescing requires its nullable `job_schedule.wake_not_before` column. Singleton notifications share a one-second window per workspace and are delayed one second; larger bursts and queue/scheduled continuations remain ungated.

### Automatic deployments

**Inbound rollout:** use `/v1/inbound` for addresses, threads, events and webhooks. For Node/Docker, stop old API and runner processes and apply migrations before starting this version. Migration `050_inbound_event_names.sql` preserves event cursors, normalizes lifecycle names and pending deliveries, and removes `message.updated` from subscriptions. Existing addresses and keys do not change. A merge does not create an SES receiving rule, change MX, verify a forwarding destination, allowlist a webhook hostname, or register a webhook. Configure those separately using the [support inbox setup](../sdk/README.md#a-support-inbox-with-tracked-replies). Before directing customer traffic to it, use an authorized external inbox to check a signed webhook, a reply, and the public From and thread in the recipient’s mail client.

**Inbound recovery (migration 084):** Apply the receiving IAM changes before cutover. From `api/`, `node --import tsx scripts/reconcile-pool-receiving.ts --apply` reapplies the Ohio lifecycle and SNS retry policy without clearing the pool’s ready state; it checks `NIGHTMAIL_PLATFORM_AWS_ACCOUNT` against STS and the existing binding. The hourly staging sweep lists `staging/` for missed notifications older than one hour. Only the object-bound SES trace establishes a recipient; unaffiliated hosts are ignored and hosts being set up can hold mail for 48 hours. Inspect `mail_receipt_holds.error`, `mail_receipts.error`, and failed `mailbox.recoverStaging` jobs. Ambiguous objects go to private `quarantine/` for 30 days when permitted. Published BYO v4 stacks need supplementary `s3:PutObject` on `quarantine/?*` and `s3:AbortMultipartUpload` on workspace raw keys; without the latter, the bucket lifecycle cleans abandoned uploads.

Select the matching hosted configuration explicitly. There is no self-hosted deployment workflow in this repository. Schema migrations run with the migration role, not as a side effect of Worker startup.

## SDK and MCP

`POST /v1/emails/preview` (`previewEmail`) previews HTML without sending mail, saving a draft or fetching remote assets. Use a read credential and supply exactly one of `html` or `emailId`, a nonempty list of distinct `clients`, and `theme` (`light` or `dark`). A stored outbound email must be visible to the caller under the same workspace, domain and content-redaction rules as `getEmailContent`; text-only or raw-only mail returns `422 EMAIL_HTML_UNAVAILABLE`. The optional `device` selects the viewport width in CSS pixels: `mobile` is 390 and `desktop` is 800. By default, iOS, Android and Samsung Email use mobile; other clients use desktop.

The response contains `previews` in the requested order. Each has `client`, `html`, and compatibility `issues`. These are approximations, not screenshots: classic Outlook returns issues only (`html: ""`), and new Outlook for Windows uses the Outlook web model without verified Windows behavior. The input limit is 256 KiB of UTF-8 HTML; document complexity and total output HTML (4 MiB) are also bounded. A limit failure returns 413 rather than partial results. **Treat preview HTML as untrusted:** call `iframe.setAttribute('sandbox', '')` (or use `<iframe sandbox="">`) before assigning `iframe.srcdoc`. Never insert the HTML into the parent page. Its embedded Content Security Policy blocks remote assets; do not remove it or grant sandbox permissions.

Set the iframe's **layout width**, not just the width of its visible crop, to the requested device preset. Pasted markup and retained HTML use the same pure functions (`emulate`, `lint` in `src/email-preview/index.ts`). They reuse Nightmail's bounded parse5 tree builder: HTML5 table repair without a browser DOM, script execution, network requests or a new dependency. Links are inert. Remote and CID images show their blocked/alt state, and fonts use local fallbacks. Color remapping and mobile fitting are approximations; standalone HTML cannot identify repeated passages in a conversation. Don't treat these previews as client screenshots or use the transform to sanitize HTML for another purpose.

An issue's `feature` is a [Can I email](https://www.caniemail.com/features/) slug: `error` means reported unsupported, `warning` means partial or unmeasured, not that the layout necessarily breaks. `lint` considers all profiles; a preview's issues describe its selected profile. Features not detected or not covered by the data have no guarantee. To refresh the vendored MIT-licensed snapshot, follow `scripts/refresh-email-support.rb` (pinned upstream checkout and content digest, no runtime fetch). The version and support notes used for each cell are in `src/email-preview/vendor/caniemail/evidence.json`.

- `/openapi.json` comes from the actual Zod route schemas; stable operation IDs define generated SDK methods. `bun run sdk` exports the contract, generates `../sdk/src`, and compiles it. Do not edit generated code. See [SDK usage](../sdk/README.md).
- The dashboard uses the same public API as integrations; OIDC establishes an administrator session, not a separate dashboard business API. API keys are hashed at rest and scoped to a workspace. New keys use `nm_<64 hex digits>` (address and inbox keys `nm_mbx_…`); existing `bane_`, `bane_mbx_`, `os_` and `os_live_` keys remain valid. Retired test keys (`os_test_` or `bane_test_`) are rejected. Requests explicitly selecting the obsolete test environment fail with `400 ENVIRONMENT_REMOVED` rather than sending real mail. A sending-domain restriction authorizes that domain and its subdomains, matching SES identity inheritance; a subdomain restriction does not authorize its parent or siblings. Give integrations only necessary grants; sending-domain restrictions are not a general data-isolation boundary.
- [MCP](../docs/mcp.md) is served at `/mcp` on this API's origin, the only MCP server. MCP clients sign in at the account issuer (`ACCOUNT_ISSUER`), which issues them their own scoped access tokens for this resource, or pass a workspace API key or agent token as the bearer. The default catalog groups operations by resource and action; `/mcp?codemode=false` (what OpenCode adds to a remote URL) lists individual operations with input and output schemas; no local process or server-side code-mode wrapper is required. A signed-in tool call acts in the workspace it names (`workspace`, else the connection's default) with the token's scopes intersected with the caller's role there, checked on every call. A key's tool calls act in its own workspace and are API requests made with that key. Writes require the corresponding permission and `confirm: true`.
- Collections return `{ data, nextCursor }`. HTTP `202` means queued, not delivered. Read stored message/event state to find the outcome.
- Domain-restricted keys can edit and preview untargeted drafts whose saved From address is on an allowed domain or subdomain. Once a campaign has an audience it is no longer visible to them. Selecting an audience, testing or launching a campaign, and reading delivery statistics require an unrestricted credential. Campaign deliveries are omitted from domain-key mail reads. Contacts, lists, segments, imports and AudienceQL also require unrestricted access (`403 UNRESTRICTED_KEY_REQUIRED`).
- This includes campaign **test** deliveries. Historical sends without recorded test provenance are visible only to unrestricted readers. A domain key may read or reuse an attachment it uploaded or one linked to direct mail it can read. Apply migration `150_attachment_uploader.sql` before deploying; no historical content is backfilled or deleted.
- Mail sent from a receiving address or inbox (replies, new conversations, forwards and mail-client submissions) is conversation mail: a reply can quote the message it answers. `GET /v1/emails`, its detail routes and Logs show it only to credentials that can read that mailbox's mail: dashboard sessions and unrestricted manage credentials. Other readers get `404` for it; their ordinary sends are unaffected. An upload that has gone out only in conversation mail follows the same rule: `GET /v1/attachments/{id}`, its content and attaching it to new mail answer `404` for those readers, unless they uploaded it or it is also attached to mail, a campaign or a review they can read.
- **Threads in Logs** (`src/thread-view.ts`). A conversation is an inbox thread: its received messages, the copy every mailbox send stores (`mail_messages.sending_email_id`), and each email sent with `POST /v1/emails/send` or `/batch` that joined it by replying. A header is no authorization: an email whose `In-Reply-To` names a stored message (`mail_message_ids`) joins that message's thread only when its sender can read the message, checked before it is admitted (`joinReplies`, which keeps those sends off the one-statement bearer-key path) and recorded as `snapshot.threadId`; the partial index `sending_emails_reply_thread` (migration 350) finds the emails that joined a thread. An email that only received replies, without answering anything, starts no thread here. `listLogs` gives each row its `threadId` and `threadCount` (counted once per thread on the page), `getEmailDetail` returns `thread` (up to 50 messages around the email, oldest first, and `count`) in its one statement, and `getInboundMessage?includeThread=true` the same for received mail. A reader sees a conversation only through the stored messages it can read one by one (receiving access); with none, it gets no thread ID, count or block, even for an email it can read. The emails that joined by replying count only for workspace credentials that read received mail, never for a key limited to addresses or an inbox. Credentials that can't read received mail get no thread.
- Webhook URLs are write-only. API and MCP responses show a masked suffix, not a usable URL. Omit `url` on a settings update to keep the endpoint, or send a full HTTPS URL to replace it. Signing secrets use their existing reveal and rotation operations.
- Use `Idempotency-Key` for retriable writes: exact repeats return the recorded result; conflicting payloads return `IDEMPOTENCY_CONFLICT`. A repeat that arrives while the first request with its key is still running gets `409 IDEMPOTENCY_IN_PROGRESS` with `Retry-After` at once; retry with the same key. Every caller of `sending_idempotency` (sends, campaigns, uploads, thread replies, internal notices and the other creates) uses this one protocol (`api/src/idempotency.ts`); operator and BYO regional routes keep their own receipts. Without a key, requests do not create idempotency records. A send's key (`sendEmail`, `sendEmailBatch`, `testCampaign`, the dashboard's test send, thread starts and replies, and internal notices) is kept at least 30 days from the request, as long as the email log: then retention deletes it with the other sent-side tables and the same key is a new request. A scheduled send's key is kept 60 days, 30 past the latest time it can go; after that a repeat is refused (`INVALID_SCHEDULE`), its time having passed. A claim still waiting for its result is never deleted. Only exactly these routes' keys expire (`SEND_PATH` in `src/maintenance.ts`). Every other key (creates, uploads, campaign launches, billing top-ups) is kept.

## IMAP and SMTP

The mail gateway (`../gateway`) serves IMAP on 993 (TLS) and 143 (STARTTLS), and SMTP on 465 (TLS) and 587 (STARTTLS), at `imap.` and `smtp.` on `MAILGW_PUBLIC_DOMAIN` (default: `PUBLIC_URL`'s host). It works only while the API has `MAILGW_API_TOKEN`: `GET /v1/me` returns the hosts as `mailServers` (null without it), and the dashboard shows them under each new app password and SMTP login, and in Docs → Keys and tokens.

- **App password** (`POST /v1/inboxes/{id}/app-passwords`): a mail app opens a persistent inbox over IMAP and SMTP. The username is the inbox address.
- **Limits** are the API's. A message may carry 25 MiB of attachments (SMTP `SIZE` is 36 MiB on the wire) and an IMAP `APPEND` may be 40 MiB, as large as received mail; the gateway streams both to the API, which parses a submission with its large files lifted out and decodes each one alone (`src/submission-mime.ts`). An envelope takes 100 recipients (RFC 5321 4.5.3.1.8) and goes to SES as emails of up to 50, To and Cc first, admitted together. A quota is only what an inbox sets (`limits`; none by default, as for received mail): over it, APPEND answers `NO [OVERQUOTA]`, and SMTP still sends the message without its Sent copy, saying so in the `250` reply. An inbox has up to 1,000 folders, 16 levels deep, and a keyword is any RFC 3501 atom; a refused folder or keyword answers `NO` with `[ALREADYEXISTS]`, `[LIMIT]` or `[CANNOT]`. A message whose top-level type is signed or encrypted (`multipart/signed`, `multipart/encrypted`, S/MIME, PGP) goes out as written, at most 8 MiB, screened by what it shows; on Hosted only from a sender past its evaluation (`422 SEALED_MAIL_UNAVAILABLE`). One forwarded as an attachment is an ordinary file.
- **SMTP login** for an app that only sends: the username `apikey` and, as the password, an API key that can do nothing but send, from `POST /v1/api-keys` with `{"permissions":["send"],"domains":["example.com"]}` or `{"access":{"type":"addresses","addressIds":["mbx_…"],"mail":["send"]}}`. It sends only from those domains (and their subdomains) or addresses, and its mail goes through `POST /v1/emails/send` with that key: the same limits, screening, billing, suppressions and events. It can't read mail; IMAP refuses it. A key that can also read or manage, or one for every domain, is refused. Revoking or narrowing the key ends its SMTP sessions at once.

How it works and how to turn it on: [gateway/PHASE2.md](../gateway/PHASE2.md#send-only-smtp-for-apps), with the [dev](../gateway/PHASE2.md#dev-turn-on-runbook) and [production](../gateway/PHASE2.md#production-turn-on-runbook) runbooks.

## Isolated sending benchmarks

**Simulated** mail always uses the full dispatch path and the real SES SDK with an in-process transport instead of AWS networking. Live mail never selects this transport. Defaults: `SIMULATED_SES_LATENCY_MS=100`, `SIMULATED_SES_RATE=1000`, and `SIMULATED_SES_DELIVERY_DELAY_MS=250`. Override the latency when modeling a different observed provider call; zero latency measures application overhead only.

Simulated dispatch verifies attachment bytes, constructs MIME, paces against the cached regional quota, claims consent/authorization, and stores acceptance atomically. It then emits two synthetic Send/Delivery callbacks that substitute for incoming SNS requests: on Cloudflare they travel the same feedback queue as verified live callbacks; on Node and in the acceptance fixture they are durable `operation.simulatedFeedback` jobs. Both paths run the same routine-ingestion statement, creating receipts when they arrive. Statuses progress through accepted/sent/delivered, but all records remain `simulated: true` and provider IDs start with `sim_`; they are not evidence of actual delivery. The public SNS endpoint still requires real signature/account verification. AWS-stored templates are explicitly rejected on simulated deployments; template discovery/rendering and AWS-side validation are not emulated. Simulated feedback uses the same receipt, status, and fan-out logic, but does not certify SNS signature verification or real mailbox delivery.

`api/scripts/benchmark-sending.ts` requires a dedicated local `bane_perf_*` database, all migrations, explicit `ENABLE_LIVE_SES=false`, and no AWS credentials. Run via `node --import tsx scripts/benchmark-sending.ts` with that isolated configuration. `BENCHMARK_RECIPIENTS` is capped at 1,000; `BENCHMARK_REPLICAS`, `BENCHMARK_LANES`, and `BENCHMARK_CONNECTIONS` control local load. Optional `BENCHMARK_REPORT` saves JSON metrics. Fixtures remain in the isolated database for inspection. Do not run it concurrently with an independent job runner. It measures the shared Node/database pipeline, not Cloudflare Queue startup or Hyperdrive latency; short high-rate runs do not establish sustained capacity.

Verified SNS callbacks never touch the sending jobs table. On Cloudflare the `/v1/events/ses` handler checks the SNS signature and account binding, tags the callback with an installation HMAC, enqueues it on `FEEDBACK_QUEUE` (`bane-<stage>-feedback-v2`) and returns 202. The tagged consumer requires a valid tag on every delivery. Failed tags, terminal ingest failures and exhausted retries go to `operation_feedback_failures` with reason `FEEDBACK_QUEUE_RECOVERY`, complete platform provenance, and lossless structured-clone payload encoding. Both native feedback DLQs have archive-only consumers and redrive storage failures to the other actively consumed DLQ; neither invokes ingestion. Only an unsigned message on the original legacy queue whose **Cloudflare `Message.timestamp`** is strictly before `LEGACY_FEEDBACK_CUTOVER` may enter the historical drain. Body timestamps, absent/malformed/future cutovers and replayed wrappers never qualify. `node --import tsx scripts/replay-feedback-quarantine.ts` lists recovery records; `--apply <ids>` rechecks the original installation tag or historical-recovery MAC and leaves unsigned/forged rows untouched. An archive signature proves the record was preserved, not that an untrusted body is valid feedback.

**Queue cutover:** apply migrations through 204 and create both v2 queues first. Fence old/foreign legacy producers, pause the old consumer, and drain in-flight old Worker invocations. Record the fixed UTC cutover in the stage config (for example `2026-11-12T18:30:00.000Z`; do not use the current time on each deployment). Then deploy the tagged producer, both normal consumers and both archive-only DLQ consumers, and resume SNS delivery. The checked-in empty value quarantines all unsigned items until the operator records the cutoff. An age cutoff does not authenticate anything an attacker already injected before it: leave it empty and recover from original SNS evidence if that historical backlog is not trusted. Once the old queue has drained, remove its original consumer, name and cutoff; retain both DLQ archive consumers until their failure destinations have been replaced by another actively drained queue. Do not reopen or advance the cutoff for replay. Stage verification keys before switching signing roots, and keep `PREVIOUS_BETTER_AUTH_SECRET` until queues, DLQs, spill storage and recovery rows have drained all old-key items.

**Historical recovery:** before acknowledging an eligible unsigned legacy item, the consumer signs a domain-separated recovery envelope covering the full body, original queue name, platform message ID, enqueue timestamp and approved cutoff. It stores that envelope as `FEEDBACK_LEGACY_RECOVERY`, then sends it to the tagged queue. A transient ingest failure and later DLQ redrive preserve that signed provenance even though the new queue timestamp changes. If re-enqueue fails, the archive remains available to `scripts/replay-feedback-quarantine.ts --apply legacy:<digest>`. Successful ingestion removes it. The old shared 10,000-row rejection cap is gone; an untrusted backlog cannot consume a trusted recovery allowance. During a PostgreSQL outage, authenticated records spill to the private attachments bucket under `feedback-recovery/v1/`; the minute cron verifies each MAC and content digest, writes the complete record/chunks to PostgreSQL, then deletes the spill object. This also handles envelopes too large for a queue. Unresolved table rows and spill objects have no automatic expiry. Alert on `FEEDBACK_RECOVERY_SPILLED`, `FEEDBACK_RECOVERY_DRAIN_FAILED`, corrupt spill records, and DLQ backlog; repair storage before the platform's queue-retention deadline. Never manufacture an old timestamp for raw legacy DLQ data or treat an archive signature as provider authentication.

The quarantine listing returns up to 500 rows and a `next` cursor; pass `--after <next>` to continue. The `untagged:`, `legacy:` and `queue:` IDs all work with `--apply`, but only authenticated underlying feedback is ingested.

Routine Delivery (and any remaining Send) callbacks are persisted by one set-based statement per batch: receipt, matched event, monotonic status (the highest-ranked new event wins when several share a batch), public event and matching webhook deliveries. Bounce, complaint, engagement and subscription callbacks are processed inline with their receipt, and messages are acknowledged or retried individually. If a callback arrives before provider acceptance is recorded, its receipt and a deferred recovery job commit together. The configuration set no longer subscribes to `SEND` events—acceptance is recorded from the `SendEmail` response—so re-run provisioning after upgrading to update the event destination. The admission rate limiter does not apply to this endpoint. On Node the handler ingests routine callbacks inline and queues non-routine ones as jobs. With no matching subscribers, no webhook job is created.

## Hosted one-click DNS (Domain Connect)

MX offers the [Nightmail mail template](../domain-connect/nightmail.dev.mail.json) (provider ID `nightmail.dev`, `domainConnectProviderIds` in `src/brand.ts`) only when the domain’s discovered provider lists it. `GET /v1/domains/{id}/dns-setup` is owner/admin dashboard only. It reads the domain once, then checks up to five DNS labels with a three-second total network budget. Each isolate caches up to 256 public results for five minutes; timeouts, rate limits and provider outages are cached for 30 seconds. Manual DNS stays available. Configure **both** `DOMAIN_CONNECT_PRIVATE_KEY` and `DOMAIN_CONNECT_KEY_ID` or neither: without them, provider information is returned without an apply URL. Incomplete settings or a non-RSA-2048 PEM disable Domain Connect and log an error; manual DNS remains available.

1. Outside the repository, generate an RSA-2048 PEM (`umask 077; openssl genrsa -out domain-connect.pem 2048`). Set the full PEM including its original newlines as the `DOMAIN_CONNECT_PRIVATE_KEY` Worker secret, and set `DOMAIN_CONNECT_KEY_ID=_dck1`. Do not use the private PEM as DNS data.
2. Publish **two separate TXT records at the same owner**, `_dck1.domainconnect.nightmail.dev.` (relative name `_dck1.domainconnect` in the `nightmail.dev` zone, created by hand). Take the base64 of the DER **SubjectPublicKeyInfo**, not the PEM text, PKCS#1 public key or a certificate. With 200-character chunks the values are `p=1,a=RS256,d=<first 200 base64 characters>` and `p=2,a=RS256,d=<remaining characters>`. No whitespace or line breaks inside a value. Print the values from the key (one record per output line), and compare them with `dig +short TXT _dck1.domainconnect.nightmail.dev` once published:

   ```sh
   openssl pkey -in domain-connect.pem -pubout -outform DER | openssl base64 -A | fold -w 200 | awk '{print "p=" NR ",a=RS256,d=" $0}'
   ```

3. Submit `nightmail.dev.mail.json` to [Domain-Connect/Templates](https://github.com/Domain-Connect/Templates) and onboard it with each DNS provider: a new provider ID is a new template to every provider, so each onboards it again. [Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dns/reference/domain-connect/): **domain-connect@cloudflare.com**, with the template link, full key TXT name, SVG logo and **DNS-only** proxy setting for DKIM CNAMEs. [Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/domains/domain-connect): **domainconnect@vercel.com**, with the template link and a short in-product demo video. [IONOS](https://www.domainconnect.org/dns-providers/): **domain_connect_admin@domain.ionos.com**, with the template link and signing-key hostname.

The [synchronous protocol](https://github.com/Domain-Connect/spec/blob/master/Domain%20Connect%20Spec%20Draft.adoc#digitally-sign-requests) uses `groupId` (comma-separated; [unselected groups stay in place](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-ietf-dconn-domainconnect-01.html#section-10.3.1)). Nightmail sorts and signs the encoded query, **excluding `key` and `sig`**, then appends those two parameters in that order. A ten-minute signed return token is also in `redirect_uri` because Cloudflare ignores `state`. The callback checks the OIDC session, the original workspace’s current role and domain ownership before restoring that workspace and redirecting to `${PUBLIC_URL}/domains/<id>`. A successful return or replay only queues the same throttled DNS/SES verification; neither grants ownership. For rotation, publish the new selector’s TXT records before switching the key pair and leave the old records through the outstanding flows’ lifetime and DNS TTL.

## Stripe setup

Hosted installations take payment for the prepaid balance through Stripe: Checkout top-ups, a saved card, invoice-backed off-session charges for saved-card top-ups and auto-recharge, and operator refunds. Stripe is only the payment rail; Nightmail keeps the balance and ledger and uses no Stripe Subscriptions, Billing or Meters. Use a Stripe account of its own for Nightmail: a shared account works (events without Nightmail's `bane_attempt` or `bane_refund` metadata are ignored) but mixes tax registrations and Radar rules. Self-hosted installations answer the billing routes with 404; a Hosted one without both keys answers the money routes with `503 BILLING_NOT_CONFIGURED`.

1. **Key.** Create a restricted key (`rk_…`): write access to Customers, Products, Checkout Sessions, Invoices (with Invoice Items), Credit Notes, Refunds and Payment Methods; read access to Payment Intents, Setup Intents, Disputes, Events and Radar early fraud warnings. Set it as the `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` Worker secret (`wrangler secret put STRIPE_SECRET_KEY --config wrangler.prod.jsonc`). A test-mode key (`rk_test_…`) only accepts test-mode events, and a live key only live ones.
2. **Webhook.** Add the endpoint `${PUBLIC_URL}/v1/billing/stripe` with API version `2026-10-28.endive` (`STRIPE_API_VERSION` in `src/billing/stripe.ts`; event payloads must match it) and these events: `checkout.session.completed`, `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded`, `checkout.session.async_payment_failed`, `checkout.session.expired`, `invoice.paid`, `invoice.payment_failed`, `payment_intent.succeeded`, `payment_intent.payment_failed`, `payment_intent.canceled`, `refund.created`, `refund.updated`, `charge.refunded`, `charge.dispute.created`, `charge.dispute.closed`, `radar.early_fraud_warning.created`, `payment_method.detached`. Set its signing secret as the `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` Worker secret.
3. **Tax.** Every top-up and recharge is a line for one Stripe Product, `STRIPE_CREDIT_PRODUCT_ID` (default `nightmail_credit`), priced before tax. Nightmail looks it up once per process and creates it when missing, with the tax code `STRIPE_TAX_CODE` (default `txcd_10103001`, SaaS for business use). An existing product is never changed: a different tax code or an archived product is logged as `BILLING_CREDIT_PRODUCT_DIFFERS`. A different tax code stays; an archived product must be unarchived by its operator before new charges resume. If Stripe refuses the product (`BILLING_CREDIT_PRODUCT_UNAVAILABLE`, usually a key without Products access), Checkout top-ups fail and charges to a saved card wait for reconciliation without turning auto-recharge off. `STRIPE_AUTOMATIC_TAX` (a plain variable, default `false`) passes `automatic_tax[enabled]` on Checkout and on every off-session invoice; Checkout then requires a billing address and saves it on the customer, offers a tax ID field, and saving a card collects and updates the customer billing address too. Stripe consults the customer shipping address, billing address, then payment-method billing details. A missing or invalid tax location fails invoice finalization with `customer_tax_location_invalid`, which turns auto-recharge off and asks the owners to update the card. Set it to `true` only once Stripe Tax (or the tax provider connected to it) is set up with its registrations; prices are shown before tax.
4. **Stripe dashboard.** Turn on **Email customers about successful payments**: receipts come from Stripe. Processing fees are absorbed.

`STRIPE_API_BASE` exists for tests only (a fake Stripe); leave it unset.

How the money moves: Nightmail writes a payment attempt, commits, then calls Stripe with an idempotency key derived from the attempt (never inside a database transaction). Credit is applied only when Stripe reports the payment (the webhook, the inline off-session charge or reconciliation) and only once per attempt. Every minute the Worker queues due auto-recharges (enforcement on only); every five minutes it reconciles attempts left open for five minutes against Stripe, re-running unanswered charges with the same keys for 22 hours, then looking the invoice up by metadata. A declined or 3DS-requiring auto-recharge turns auto-recharge off and emails the owners once; there is no automatic retry. Alert on `BILLING_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, `BILLING_DISPUTE_OPENED`, `BILLING_EARLY_FRAUD_WARNING`, `BILLING_LATE_PAYMENT` and `BILLING_CREDIT_PRODUCT_UNAVAILABLE` / `BILLING_CREDIT_LINE_UNAVAILABLE` (errors) and `BILLING_WEBHOOK_FAILED`, `BILLING_RECHARGE_FAILED`, `BILLING_RECONCILE_FAILED` and `BILLING_CREDIT_PRODUCT_DIFFERS` (warnings).

## SES and production release gates

With `ENABLE_LIVE_SES=false` or missing AWS credentials, all keys use the simulator. SES discovery and provisioning return `503 SES_NOT_CONFIGURED`. A campaign records its launch mode. If the worker mode changes before its run finishes, the run stops and the campaign fails with `SES_MODE_CHANGED`. ### Region discovery and explicit setup API

These are authenticated public API operations, not separate dashboard-only endpoints. Region access requires an unrestricted principal (no sending-domain restriction); AWS reads require `read` access, and configuration/provisioning require `manage` access.

| Operation | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /v1/regions` | `read` access: database catalog of default/enabled regions, cached discovery status, and provision job status/errors. Never calls AWS. |
| `PUT /v1/regions/{region}` | Body `{ "enabled": true }` and/or `{ "makeDefault": true }`. Disabling the default, a region with queued/in-flight/uncertain mail or scheduled/sending campaigns, or active provisioning is blocked. Disabling deletes no AWS resources and preserves trusted feedback for historical mail. |
| `GET /v1/regions/{region}/discovery` | Enable the region first. | `POST /v1/regions/{region}/provision` | Body `{ "confirm": true }`. Returns `202 { jobId, status }`, reusing an active job. Setup manages only resources tagged with this installation's `bane:installation-id` and `bane:purpose=ses-feedback`; it never automatically adopts manually created resources. Name/ownership collisions and missing permissions produce coded blockers, not permission escalation. SNS publishing is scoped to the verified account and the two configuration-set ARNs. The subscription uses signed SNS JSON with `RawMessageDelivery=false`; only the existing signature-verified callback confirms subscriptions for trusted topics. There is no public `SubscribeURL` trust override. See [SNS delivery verification](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sns/latest/dg/sns-verify-signature-of-message.html).

The managed destinations enable `SEND`, `DELIVERY`, `BOUNCE`, `COMPLAINT`, `REJECT`, `RENDERING_FAILURE`, `DELIVERY_DELAY`, `OPEN`, and `CLICK`. Open/click tracking still follows each message’s explicit tracking override; `tracking: false` disables it. `SUBSCRIPTION` is not enabled because Nightmail owns consent. Setup does not change DNS, obtain SES production/sandbox approval, mutate IAM, delete unrelated resources, add SQS/Lambda, or request mailbox access. Unrelated event destinations/subscriptions remain unchanged.

## Sending and operational boundaries

**Scheduled single sends.** `POST /v1/emails/send` takes `scheduledAt`, an RFC 3339 instant with `Z` or an offset (a local time without one is refused; times come back in UTC), from a minute ago (a sender's clock; it then goes at once) to 30 days ahead, the email log's retention, so a waiting email is visible the whole time. Longer plans are campaigns (a year). Batches and tracked conversation sends (`replyAddressId`) go now. Up to 100,000 may wait per workspace (`429 SCHEDULED_EMAIL_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`). The email is stored at once (`sending_emails.status = 'scheduled'`, which the dispatcher never leases) with a `scheduled` event and one release job (`sending.release`, `release:<email id>`) due at its time; `scheduledAt` and the bounds are checked in `send_admission` after the idempotency claim, so an exact retry replays its receipt however late. `getEmail` and `listEmails` show it as `queued` with `scheduled: true` (Email's statuses are a published enum); `listEmails?status=scheduled` lists those still waiting, and `listLogs` calls them `scheduled`. Until its release starts, `POST /v1/emails/{id}/reschedule` (`rescheduleEmail`, a `rescheduled` event; the job is requeued with a fresh run token, as a campaign's run is) and `POST /v1/emails/{id}/cancel` (`cancelEmail`: `canceled`, `EMAIL_CANCELED`, a `canceled` event and `email.rejected`) apply; after, `409 EMAIL_NOT_SCHEDULED`. Scheduling charges nothing: at its time the release job admits it with `send_admission` as a send made then, under the email's row lock (standing and holds, the Hosted daily limit, the balance, the dispatch queue, and the sender's region and binding again), and moves it to `queued` with `created_at` set to that moment, so the hourly Hosted window and the billing meter charge that hour (`hosted_charged` treats `scheduled` as uncharged, migration 344). A refusal then rejects it with the refusal's code (`SEND_LIMIT_REACHED`, `BALANCE_EXHAUSTED`, `HOSTED_SENDING_PAUSED`, `MARKETING_HELD`, `SENDER_DOMAIN_NOT_VERIFIED`, …): status `rejected`, a `reject` event with `providerCalled: false` and the `email.rejected` webhook, nothing charged; reschedule or send it again. Hosted marketing whose ramp hour has no room (`RAMP_LIMIT_REACHED`), or that a quarantined campaign pauses (`RAMP_PAUSED`), is held, not rejected, until it may go (docs/sending-limits.md); scheduling it is refused past a day of the ramp's step. Only a full dispatch queue (`PENDING_EMAIL_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`) or Hosted setup finishing (`TENANT_NOT_READY`) is waited out, retried every 30 seconds for up to 15 minutes past its time. The release is one transaction and the dispatcher's claim sends a queued row once, so a crash or a second runner sends it at most once. A release job never ends leaving its email waiting: when its last attempt fails, or its last lease runs out (the job runner's claim, `failScheduledRelease` in `src/states.ts`), the email is rejected with `SCHEDULE_RELEASE_FAILED`, a `reject` event and `email.rejected`, under the email's row lock and only while the job still carries that attempt's run token, read after the lock; if even that can't be written, the release starts over.

`202` means queued, not delivered. The default `email.rejected` webhook now also reports local terminal outcomes: read `data.status` (`suppressed`, `canceled`, `acceptance_unknown`) and `data.code`. A refusal by the content screen is `data.code` `CONTENT_REJECTED_SPAM` and nothing more: the screen's score, its model and its threshold are never in an email's events, a webhook payload or the webhook delivery log, for new refusals or for stored ones (`withoutVerdict` in `src/core.ts`). They stay in the stored event's `data.screen`, which only the operator timeline reads (the score and the threshold, for staff), and are removed where the published event and its webhook payloads are built from it (`terminalEvents` in `src/states.ts`) and on every read and post. The support agent's reads get the refusal in words. **Do not resend `acceptance_unknown`: SES may have accepted it.** Admission allows 15 final headers including threading and the two marketing unsubscribe headers; attachment MIME types and content IDs are limited to 78 ASCII characters.

`email.bounced` includes the SES bounce type and, when the receiving server gave one, its reply in `data.diagnosticCode`; `email.delivery_delayed` includes the reply, SES's `delayType` and `expirationTime` (when SES stops retrying). The reply is the receiving server's text, so read it as data. A webhook payload, and the webhook delivery log, carry it redacted: the addresses and IP addresses in it are replaced (`<address>`, `<ip>`) and it is cut at 160 characters. The replacement works by pattern on what mail servers write, so treat the text as possibly personal all the same. The email's own events (`getEmailDetail`, `listEmailEvents`) keep the reply as SES reported it, up to 500 characters on one line. A bounce SES generates itself (its suppression list, its address validation) keeps no reply. Only `Permanent` counts as a hard bounce; SES validation drops remain separate. Other bounces are recorded as `non_permanent_bounce` and cannot replace a delivered status. Only explicit `not-spam` complaint feedback (ignoring case and surrounding whitespace) is recorded as `complaint_feedback` without suppression or a complaint alert. These rules apply to new feedback; they do not clear prior suppressions.

Monitor `SES_FEEDBACK_QUARANTINED`: migration 087 retains isolated, deterministically invalid callbacks in `operation_feedback_failures` for 30 days. Its `payload` is private JSON text. Database outages and persistent lock conflicts return the message to the queue instead. A job whose final running lease expires remains inspectable as `failed` with `JOB_LEASE_EXHAUSTED`; the associated campaign or webhook delivery fails with it. A campaign cancellation takes effect immediately; its email records and webhook deliveries settle in pages of 1,000.

### Webhook request headers

To authenticate an email or inbound webhook, include `"headers": [{"name":"Authorization","value":"Bearer <endpoint-token>"}]` in `POST /v1/webhooks` or `POST /v1/inbound/webhooks`. On `PATCH`, omitting `headers` preserves them only if the URL stays the same. Changing the URL clears old headers unless you supply replacements. Send `[]` to remove all headers. Current headers accompany every attempt, including retries, redelivery and email webhook tests. The signing protocol is unchanged.

Header names must be unique HTTP tokens (case-insensitive, 64 characters maximum); values must be HTTP field bytes (tab, visible ASCII or Latin-1 above 0x7F), at most 2,048 characters. Up to 10 headers and 8 KiB total (UTF-8 names + values). `Host`, `Content-Length`, `Content-Type`, `Content-Encoding`, `Transfer-Encoding`, `Connection`, `Keep-Alive`, `TE`, `Trailer`, `Upgrade`, `HTTP2-Settings`, `Expect`, `Proxy-*`, `User-Agent`, `Webhook-Id`, `Webhook-Timestamp`, `Webhook-Signature` and `Nightmail-Test` (also legacy `Bane-Test` and `OpenSend-Test`) are reserved. `Cookie` is also reserved so a server-to-server callback cannot forward a browser session cookie; use endpoint-specific credentials instead.

Migration `070_webhook_headers.sql` adds nullable encrypted header storage; existing endpoints send no extra headers. Header values are write-only. Managers see `{name,hint,updatedAt}` (a masked suffix, fully masked for short values); unrestricted read API keys see `{name}`. Keys limited to addresses cannot inspect workspace endpoints. No custom headers are stored in delivery histories.

### Errors, logs, and retention

Errors use `{ error: { code, message, requestId, retryable, field?, required? } }` and an `x-request-id` header.

A permission refusal (403) keeps its code and adds `required`, what the request needs, and the same in a `Nightmail-Required-Permission` header; its message ends with a sentence naming the permission. Each part is present only when it applies:

| Field | Values |
| --- | --- |
| `permission` | `sendEmail`, `sentMail`, `receivedMail`, `inboxes`, `audience` (audience and content), `receivingRules`, `configuration` |
| `level` | `on` (for `sendEmail`), `read` or `write`; with `person` alone, what the person's MCP sign-in must be approved for |
| `scope` | what the credential's scope must cover: `workspace` (no sender-domain limit), `allAddresses`, `domain:<name>` or `address:<address>` |
| `credential` | `dashboard`, `signIn` (an MCP sign-in), `lasting` (not a temporary agent token) or `notDelegated` (a dashboard session, or an API key no MCP approval made) |
| `person` | a person, never a key (a dashboard session or their own MCP sign-in), with role `admin` (an owner or admin) or `owner`. The workspace, its members, invitations and billing are a person's, so their refusals name only this |

For example `{ "permission": "receivedMail", "level": "read", "scope": "workspace" }` is sent as `Nightmail-Required-Permission: received_mail=read; scope="workspace"`. Over MCP the tool error carries the same `required`. Search JSON logs by that ID; dispatch carries it through the outbox. Logs include operation/job/attempt/error codes, not request bodies, credentials, or unsubscribe tokens. Unexpected stack traces remain server-side. API-key last-use timestamps update at most once per minute.

Every operation states what it needs in the same terms, before any request: `x-nightmail-permission` in `/openapi.json` (`src/operation-permissions.ts`), with `text`, the sentence the docs show; MCP `describe_tool` returns it as `permission`. There `scope` names what must be covered (`workspace`, `domain` or `address`), `person` alone marks an operation that only people call (`any` is every member) and `level` beside it what their MCP sign-in must be approved for, `delegatedLevel` is the level an MCP sign-in or agent token needs where an API key needs less, `withinCaller` marks a request that also names access it gives and is checked to give no more than the caller has (an agent token, an inbox and its key), and `access` is `public` (no credential), `staff` (support and operator tooling) or `any` (any credential of the workspace). Each row records what the guards enforce today; the acceptance test `permission table` checks every row against REST, and every scoped row on real resources inside and outside a limited key's sender domain or addresses.

Detailed email/event/webhook logs expire after 30 days, and so do sends' idempotency keys (at least 30 days from the request; 60 for a scheduled send). Retention (`src/maintenance.ts`) runs within a hard budget (5 minutes hourly, 2 for the 15-minute sent-side run; no statement starts after it), gives each table its own share of that time and its own failure, deletes oldest rows first, and rests between full batches as long as each took. A run that ends before every table starts the next run at the first it didn't reach (`retention_state`). Its `RETENTION_COMPLETED` or `RETENTION_PARTIAL` line lists, per table, the rows removed, the age in seconds of the oldest row still waiting, the tables that failed and the tables it didn't reach; a failed table also appears on `MAINTENANCE_CLEANUP_FAILED`. Active jobs, campaign drafts, engagement, consent/suppression, other idempotency results, and valid unsubscribe links have separate lifecycles. Unreferenced old attachment metadata is removed transactionally; object deletion is queued durably. Failed deletion jobs retain object keys for recovery. Contact deletion is **not** a promise to purge all audit/safety records, imported copies, or campaign revisions.

**List counts.** A list's member counts (`GET /v1/lists`, `GET /v1/lists/{id}`) are kept in `audience_list_counts` by triggers on memberships and contacts (migration 405), in the transaction of each change, so reading them does not count members; nothing that decides who receives an email reads them. Retention folds each list's rows into one hourly. Once a day the hourly upkeep counts every list's memberships and compares: `AUDIENCE_LISTS_RECOUNTED` summarises the run, and a difference is logged as `AUDIENCE_LIST_COUNTS_DRIFT` at error level with the workspace, the list and the difference, then corrected. A difference is a bug in a write path, never routine: look for a statement that changes a contact's consent or suppression and its memberships at once, or a write made with the triggers disabled. `SELECT * FROM reconcile_audience_list_counts('<workspace>', '<list>')` recounts one list by hand and returns what it corrected (no row when the counts were right); it reads only, and no write waits for it.

### Security boundaries

Hosted daily limits follow the sender score ([how sending limits work](../docs/sending-limits.md)): 500 recipients per rolling 24 hours during the evaluation (the first 500 real recipients, until their delivery results are in, about an hour), then none at a score of 75 or more (good standing) and 500, 200 or 100 under it; a ladder step from warn up, a pause or a resume freezes the limit (none becomes what SES accepted in the last 24 hours, at least 10,000: `hosted_freeze`). Without a daily limit, `hosted_minute_cap` paces a workspace at a quarter of the pool account's SES rate a minute, at least 500, and for 24 clean hours after the evaluation the growth pace (`hosted_growth`, charged in `hosted_burst_ok`) allows a sponsor's growing workspaces 1,000 recipients a rolling hour, raised to twice the distinct recipients of each feedback-mature clean hour; `POST /v1/operator/workspaces/{id}/growth/lift` lifts it. The ten-minute standing job (`src/standing.ts`, elected from the minute cron and the Node runner; the hourly maintenance runs it too) stores each workspace's score, limit and ladder step in `sender_standing`, and admission reads them in its existing statement. Default daily and minute budgets are shared by workspaces created by the same approved account, even after an ownership transfer. Explicit operator overrides remain per workspace and may expire. A workspace the job has not seen yet gets the evaluation's limit (`EVALUATION` in `src/standing-model.ts`, next to `GROWTH` with the growth pace's hours: the only definitions); `HOSTED_SEND_CAP_FIRST_WEEK` and the retired second-week and mature settings (`HOSTED_SEND_CAP_SECOND_WEEK`, `HOSTED_SEND_CAP_MATURE`) are no longer read. Live SES mailbox-simulator recipients consume capacity but never count as evidence. Suppressed mail and local simulated sends consume no daily quota. Under a daily limit, marketing may use the limit minus max(100, 10%) of it (overrides keep the plain `DISPATCH_DAILY_HEADROOM_PERCENT` reserve); during the evaluation it may use all of it. Campaigns wait for capacity.

Inbound defaults per workspace are 50,000 messages and 20 GiB an hour, refilled continuously, with a backlog of 20,000; mail over them waits for the next free slot (up to 15 minutes ahead), and only a flood past that waits for the next hour. Operators can set workspace-specific values in `mail_receive_limits`. MIME with excessive headers (64 KiB or 256 header lines), deep nesting, or an exhausted cooperative one-second parser budget remains available as raw mail. Inbound event long polls reserve one request-budget unit per requested second in addition to ordinary admission.
The reputation ladder runs with the standing job: notice, warn (limit frozen, owners emailed), restrict (marketing held with 409 `MARKETING_HELD`, campaigns pause, transactional mail flows) and pause (409 `HOSTED_SENDING_PAUSED` on admission and dispatch). Each line needs a count and a rate over the last 24 hours of hard bounces and complaints per destination against 48 hours of accepted volume, or 7 days against 8; the thresholds are in [sending-limits.md](../docs/sending-limits.md). Bounces alone hold marketing; they pause only when transactional mail itself bounces 5% and 50. Content-screen verdicts are counted when the screen judges a message, the dispatcher's or, for a campaign recipient's copy, its release's (`hosted_screen_hourly`, real recipients only, each recipient once), so spam admitted before a resume still counts after it. A pause queues `hosted.tenantStatus`, which sets the workspace's SES tenants to `DISABLED` (`UpdateReputationEntityCustomerManagedStatus`) as a backstop. Queued mail of a workspace paused for 72 hours is canceled (`ACCOUNT_PAUSED`). Every change of standing writes `standing_events`, publishes `account.standing.changed`, and at warn and above emails owners and admins (**Account emails**, below). Inspect `STANDING_CHANGED` logs, `connection.standing` on `GET /v1/me` and `standing` on `GET /v1/sender-score`.

**Built-in domains** (hosted, `src/builtin-domain.ts`, migration 495). With `NIGHTMAIL_BUILTIN_DOMAIN` set, every workspace gets `<label>.<parent>` (`acme-k7.nightmail.email`): its name in DNS letters, a protected brand or reserved word replaced by `mail`, plus 2 to 4 random characters. It is allocated when the workspace is created, or by the backfill in `domainSetupTick`, and is never given out again. With the workspace's Hosted connection it becomes an ordinary claim marked `identity.builtin` on that connection in Ohio. It sends with the workspace's own SES tenant under the parent identity, which the `domain.builtin` job associates with that tenant, so DKIM signs as the parent and MAIL FROM is the parent's. It receives on its own name through the pool's catch-all receipt rule, into a default inbox (`hello@`) by a `*@` catch-all route, and spam and viruses to it are dropped before they are counted. Its proof is the parent identity's, checked hourly; the per-domain DNS jobs skip it. It can't be deleted or changed and counts toward no domain limit. `send_admission` keeps it to transactional email sent now, without attachments or campaigns, within `NIGHTMAIL_BUILTIN_SEND_SCOPE`, screened as a new sender's, until the workspace verifies a domain of its own (`OWN_DOMAIN_VERIFIED`). `node --import tsx scripts/builtin-domain.ts revoke <ws_id> --apply --reason "…"` turns one off for good (abuse). A circuit breaker watches the parent's reputation: when all built-in domains' mail together reaches 5% permanently bounced recipients or 0.1% complaints (one per reported email; at least 1,000 real recipients and 3 complaints, over 48 hours, hourly), and still does without the workspace contributing most, every built-in domain stops sending (mail still queued from them is canceled; receiving continues, `/v1/me` shows the built-in domain `off` with `paused: true`), a `BUILTIN_PARENT_TRIPPED` card goes to `REPUTATION_ALERT_SLACK_URL`, and it stays tripped until an operator runs `node --import tsx scripts/builtin-domain.ts reset-breaker --apply --reason "…"`, after which only newer mail counts. When one workspace alone puts built-in mail over a line, nothing trips: a `BUILTIN_WORKSPACE_OVER_LINE` card names it (at most daily), to revoke it. The parent's own name receives only `abuse@`, `postmaster@`, `dmarc@` and `tls-reports@` (its DMARC and TLS-RPT report addresses), for the platform workspace (`NIGHTMAIL_PLATFORM_DOMAIN_WORKSPACE`), once an operator runs `node --import tsx scripts/builtin-domain.ts apex --route abuse=mbx_… --route dmarc=mbx_… [--apply --reason "…"]`: a receiving host for the parent under that workspace's built-in domain and an exact route for each role address. Mail to any other address at the parent is dropped. The parent is never claimed (its claim would overlap every built-in domain's) and never sends. The runtime key may associate only the tagged parent with a tenant (`infra/aws-shared/runtime-ohio-policy.json`).

**Account emails.** Every email a customer gets about their account is written by `src/notices.ts` (`customerNotice`): three short paragraphs (what happened, what it means for mail now, what to do), one link and a line saying why it was sent, as a text part and a small HTML part with the same words. None says how a check works: no rates, thresholds, scores, provider names or error codes; the dashboard has those. They go from `NIGHTMAIL_NOTIFY_FROM` through the platform workspace (`sendSystemEmail`), to the workspace's owners and admins (up to 20). Words a customer chose appear only where a template puts them and only as plain words: a workspace's or campaign's name through `shownName` (no address, URL or host, no look-alike letters, 40 characters), a domain as a host name, an address as a plain address. The invitation, which goes to an address outside the workspace, has a fixed subject and names its inviter by address only.

- **One event, one email.** A warning's and a campaign pause's email wait 15 minutes (`NOTICE_WAIT_MINUTES`). The campaign's is then not sent when marketing was held or the workspace paused from 15 minutes before it; the warning's is not when a campaign paused from 30 minutes before it, or the workspace was held or paused after it. A pause and a marketing hold are always sent, at once.
- **Never twice.** A standing email's key is its `standing_events` row, a billing email's its `billing_notices` row, and the others' an `account_notices` row (migration 403, unique `dedupe_key`), queued with its job in the statement that makes it. The job leases the row, sends under that key (a retry is the same email; a key an earlier build used with other words counts as sent) and settles it `sent`, `suppressed` (the moment passed: verified again, revoked, paused meanwhile) or `failed` (nobody to tell, no sender, or every attempt failed).
- **A send that fails is retried.** It gives the row's lease back, so the job's next attempts (six, over about an hour and a half) send it; a job that finds the row still leased by a run that died waits for that lease to end instead of finishing. When the last attempt fails, or the send's outcome is unknown and sending again could make two, the row is `failed` and `NOTICE_SEND_FAILED` is logged as an error with the workspace and kind: that customer was not told. A standing email has no row to lease: its job fails and retries as any job does, and its last failure is the job's own error line (`standing.notify`). Queuing never fails what it reports: an insert that fails is logged (`NOTICE_NOT_QUEUED`).
- **The waitlist email** (`waitlistAdmitted` in `src/account-notices.ts`) goes to each address a raise of the sign-up limit lets in, once (`placesOpened` in `src/signups.ts`).
- **A sign-in link** (`signinLink` in `src/account-notices.ts`) goes to an address with a held place when it is asked for (`sendSigninLink` in `src/signups.ts`); one used or expired before its job runs is not sent.
- **Pictures:** `node --import tsx scripts/render-notices.ts <dir>` then `node scripts/notice-shots.mjs <dir> <shots>` in `app/` draws every email in Apple Mail, Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo Mail and Samsung Email, light and dark, with the product's own client emulation.

**What counts as marketing** is decided by Nightmail, not only by the caller's `kind`: a message labelled transactional with 10+ real recipients, list headers (`List-Id`, other `List-*`, `Precedence: bulk/list/junk`) or 10+ identical copies in one request is stamped `snapshot.bulk` and treated as marketing for holds, the transactional reserve, the dispatch fence and reputation attribution (`hosted-sending.ts` `bulkFlags`). It keeps its stream and content. Internal: not in the public docs.

An **exempt (first-party) workspace** (`set-workspace-exemption.ts`, operator-only and audited) is never held or paused by the ladder: its standing is computed as usual but stops at warn, so it keeps sending transactional and marketing mail. Crossing a restrict or pause line warns: the owner email and `account.standing.changed` at warn, and a `reputation.alert` to the operators (`HOSTED_EXEMPT_OVER_LINE`, `REPUTATION_ALERT_SLACK_URL`, once per line a day). Only an operator pauses it, with a reason (the operator API, or `scripts/pause-hosted-sending.ts`). AWS's own tenant pauses still apply, and alert the operators too.

**The customer share.** Outside workspaces on a shared account (Hosted, not exempt) together may use `HOSTED_CUSTOMER_SHARE_PERCENT` (default 50) of that account and region's daily SES limit in a rolling 24 hours, so they can never crowd out first-party mail. What they used is SES's own count for the account and region (`SentLast24Hours`, read with every quota sample) less the real recipients SES accepted there for exempt workspaces in the hours admission counts (`hosted_customer_day`, migration 401), so anything else on the account counts as theirs. The dispatcher's elected quota refresh works it out once a minute per account and region and stores it beside the sample (`provider_account_limits.customer_sent_24_hours`); no send or release sums anything. Once it is used up their marketing waits as it does for the SES daily reserve: queued mail stays queued, and campaigns hold with `waiting.reason` `hosted_daily_quota` ("Waiting for Hosted sending capacity"). Both resume by themselves on a later sample, as the window rolls or when SES raises the limit. Their transactional mail still sends, and exempt workspaces and BYO accounts are never counted or held. Each refresh that finds the share used up logs `HOSTED_CUSTOMER_SHARE_FULL` (error: account, region, `used`, `share`, `percent`). Where it stands now: `SELECT aws_account_id, region, max_24_hour_send, customer_sent_24_hours, checked_at FROM provider_account_limits`. To change it, set the variable in `wrangler.prod.jsonc` and deploy: each dispatcher applies it from its next run, so lowering it below what they used holds their marketing at once.

Nightmail is the source of truth for the tenant status. Every pause and resume bumps `provider_connections.tenant_generation`, and `hosted_tenant_status` records what was applied to each regional tenant at which generation. The job applies what Nightmail wants now, region by region, and runs again if a transition happened meanwhile. A disable is journaled (`DISABLING`) before AWS is called and `ENABLED` is recorded only after AWS confirms, so an outcome lost between AWS and the database counts as possibly disabled and a resume enables it again; every standing run queues any workspace still out of line (`tenantStatusDrift`), including a tenant that became ready after the pause. The runtime IAM policy must allow `ses:UpdateReputationEntityCustomerManagedStatus` on the workspace tenants; until it does, the job logs `SES_TENANT_STATUS_DENIED` and records nothing (the database hold applies either way).

After reviewing the cause, an operator resumes through the operator API (below) or with `DATABASE_URL` set runs `node --import tsx scripts/resume-hosted-sending.ts WORKSPACE_ID --reason "why"` from `api/`. Queued mail can dispatch again, and only events after the resume count toward another pause. A resume keeps the score and limit and records a strike. For 48 hours the limit is min(current limit, 50% of the limit before the resume), operator overrides included; it is stored as an absolute cap, so a second resume in the window cannot raise it (`nextCap`/`nextCapAt` on `GET /v1/me` say when the rest returns). `--restore 100` (`restore: 100` in the API) opts out and is audited. A pause whose SES tenants were disabled stays held, with a "resuming" reason, until the tenant job has enabled them again. A marketing hold placed by the hourly check before the standing shipped (`provider_connections.marketing_held_at`) becomes a restrict on the next standing run. Content-screen rejections count against the daily limit like sends; other rejections are refunded.

**Operators (Nightmail staff).** Platform roles (`operator`, `operator:read`) live in `platform_operators`, never in a workspace: grant one with `node --import tsx scripts/set-operator.ts EMAIL operator --reason "why"` (`off` removes it; the user must have signed in once). Operators use the `/operator` area of the dashboard, backed by `/v1/operator/*` (hidden from the public OpenAPI document and SDK): the review queue, sign-ups (the sign-up limit and every workspace with its owner, verified domain, sends and standing), the waitlist, a workspace page, the evidence timeline, pause, resume (50% or 100%), marketing hold, campaign release, limit overrides with an expiry (an override ends a resume's reduced limit), and the audit log. **Lift a bounce suppression (operator only).** A hard bounce or validation drop suppresses the address for the workspace with no public way back, which locks a person out of sign-in mail even after their mailbox exists. With a short-lived operator DB role and AWS credentials for the Hosted pool account, run `node --import tsx scripts/lift-suppression.ts WORKSPACE_ID ADDRESS --reason "..."` from `api/`. Non-manage readers receive app unsubscribe capabilities redacted from content and nested event/webhook payloads; raw MIME is withheld. This does not sanitize arbitrary third-party password-reset links—content-read grants belong only with trusted integrations. Campaign substitutions reject executable, unquoted, comment, and foreign-markup contexts and validate completed URLs. SES templates retain their caller-escaping contract.

Postgres request/resource budgets bound major amplification paths. Authenticated credentials default to 1,200 requests/minute. Outstanding (queued or attempting) direct email is bounded per workspace at a day of what it can send, between 10,000 and 200,000: the account's SES 24-hour quota with bring-your-own AWS, and on the shared pool a day of the workspace's pace in good standing (a quarter of the pool's SES rate a minute, at least 500, so 200,000 in practice). During the evaluation, the growth pace or under a daily limit a shared-pool workspace keeps 10,000; an operator override (`scripts/set-hosted-limits.ts --pending-cap`) or an exemption (200,000) replaces either. It is a queue bound, not a volume limit: queued rows are the dispatch queue, so the bound keeps one workspace's backlog within a day of dispatch and the table bounded; a refused submission (`429 PENDING_EMAIL_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`) can be retried as the queue drains. There is no per-key cap. Stored attachments are limited to 100 GiB per workspace (uploads are kept as long as their emails, 30 days) and expanded batch content to 128 MiB. These are implementation limits, not plan entitlements or full isolation from shared infrastructure costs.

Coarse admission runs before DB access: 6,000 requests/minute per IPv4 or IPv6 /64 per Node process or Cloudflare location (approximate). SNS has a separate Cloudflare lane at the same limit, and its signature is verified before looking up the topic. An isolate/process reserves up to 40 public credential attempts per peer per minute before dispatch; successful authentication refunds the reservation. Public auth entry points have their own 40/minute lane. Node does not trust forwarded-IP headers. Use correctly configured proxy/WAF controls for distributed abuse. Cloudflare invocation URL logs/traces are disabled; review every external log sink independently.

## Audit trail

Every account has one append-only trail, `audit_events` (migration 260): who acted (a member, an API key, an agent (its MCP sign-in or an agent token), an operator, a support conversation, Nightmail itself or Stripe), in which workspace and for which user, the action (`api_key.create`, `member.role_change`, `domain.delete`, `support.view`…), the target, a before/after summary, the reason and ticket, the request ID, and the client's network (/24 or /48) and user-agent family. Summaries never hold secrets (key values, hashes, passwords, webhook secrets or URLs) or message content.

- **Immutable in SQL:** triggers refuse `UPDATE`, `TRUNCATE` and any `DELETE` before `retain_until` for every role, the superuser included. A role that owns the table (or holds DDL on it) can still disable the triggers, so the API must connect as a non-owner role with `SELECT`, `INSERT` and `DELETE` (the purge) only; check it with `SELECT privilege_type FROM information_schema.role_table_grants WHERE table_name='audit_events' AND grantee=current_user`. The hourly maintenance purge deletes expired rows in pages.
- **Retention:** 1 year by default (it covers card dispute windows and an annual review), 7 years for billing and payment events, 90 days for the per-call log of a support conversation.
- **Writers:** `recordAudit(tx, c, event)` inside the change's own transaction, or `auditInsert(...)` composed into the change's single statement (no extra round trip). Send admission never writes here; sending activity is in the sending logs. Operator scripts, the operator API, hosted pauses and limit overrides reach the trail through SQL triggers (migration 262).
- **Readers:** owners and admins see their workspace's customer-visible rows in Settings → Activity (`GET /v1/activity`), or over their own MCP sign-in approved for manage (`settings` · `activity`); API keys and agent tokens never read it. Operators see everything through `GET /v1/operator/workspaces/{id}/audit` and `GET /v1/operator/users/{id}/audit` (filter by `since`, `until`, `action`, `actorType`, `actorId`, `requestId`; cursor pages; `include=enforcement,billing` joins the standing events and billing ledger where those tables exist). Each operator read is itself written to the trail.

## Support integration

Internal: these routes serve Nightmail's operators and its support platform, never customers. `/v1/operator/*` answers only an operator and `/v1/support/*` only a support conversation, through the support platform's binding; every customer credential (any dashboard role, API keys, MCP grants, agent tokens, inbox keys, app passwords) gets the same `403` with no data on every path there, real or not. Neither appears as an MCP tool, and both are hidden (`hide: true`): they are not in `/openapi.json`, the SDK, `/docs`, `/docs.md` or `/llms.txt`. The support platform calls `/v1/support/*` through its `SupportPlatform` service binding, never the SDK.

**The support platform's access: a service binding, no token.** The support Worker (`support/`, `nightmail-support` / `nightmail-support-dev`) calls the `SupportPlatform` entrypoint this Worker exports (`src/worker.ts`, logic in `src/support-platform.ts`) over a Cloudflare service binding in the same account: `{ "binding": "BANE", "service": "nightmail-production", "entrypoint": "SupportPlatform" }`. Only a Worker whose deployed config declares that binding can call it, so there is nothing to leak or rotate; removing the binding or the entrypoint revokes it. Its methods are the whole surface: `verifyHandoff(token)` (Nightmail checks its own signature, audience, issuer and expiry, then the stored row: used or not, the person's message), `registerConversation`, `closeConversation`, `request(conversationId, method, path, body?)` (below) and the inbox's `mirrorMessages`, `mirrorState`, `inboxState`, `pullOutbox` and `markDelivery`. They run through the normal routes (validation, audit rows, budgets) as the operator `service:bane-support`, on their own admission lane, and only on `/v1/operator/support/{handoffs/{id}, conversations, conversations/{id}/close, conversations/{id}/messages, conversations/{id}/state, conversations/{id}/messages/{messageId}/delivery, outbox}`; `request()` reaches `/v1/support/*` for one conversation. Every other operator route refuses it, and no bearer token is an operator. (`NIGHTMAIL_SUPPORT_PLATFORM_TOKEN` is retired: delete the secret from any Worker that still has it.) Support grants are gone (migration 375): verifying an email sender, minting, listing and revoking grants, and the staff sign-in for the support Worker's old ticket pages answer `404`; staff use the dashboard's Support inbox.

**The flow.** The agent never chooses or asserts who the customer is: Nightmail decides, from evidence it checks itself, and the support Worker only names the conversation (**Support conversations**, below).

1. **In-app:** "Contact support" calls `POST /v1/contact-support` (dashboard session, any role) with the page, recent request IDs, an optional message and an off-by-default `shareContent`. Nightmail stores a handoff (`sho_…`) that opens **one** conversation within **30 minutes**, and returns the support URL with an EdDSA JWS (10 minutes, `aud: bane-support`, claims: workspace, user, role, page, request IDs, `shareContent`). The platform verifies it with `verifyHandoff` (or the public keys) and reads it back with `GET /v1/operator/support/handoffs/{id}` (which also shows whether it was used, and the conversation it opened).
2. **Register:** the platform registers the chat with that handoff (`registerConversation`). It covers the handoff's person and workspace; using the handoff marks it used, so a second registration gets `409 SUPPORT_HANDOFF_USED`.
3. **By email:** the platform registers each message received at a support address. Nightmail binds the conversation to the sender's account only from that message's own evidence (below), or from the sender's signed-in confirmation of that one message (the claim); otherwise it is bound to nobody and reads nothing.
4. **Use:** every `/v1/support/*` read goes through `request(conversationId, …)`, which derives the customer from the conversation's row; `workspaceId` can only narrow it to one of its workspaces (those the person still belongs to). A chat reads, chooses its workspace, keeps notes and proposes actions; a bound email conversation only reads the account's status, domains and sending, and narrows its workspace (`POST /v1/support/scope`): the notes, actions and DNS re-check routes, billing, the audit trail, request logs and webhook deliveries refuse it (`403 SUPPORT_CHAT_ONLY`, nothing charged or logged), its context leaves out the activity trail, webhook failures, request IDs and the API-key count (same schema, empty), and it lists and opens sent mail only. The Help chat reads all of them, by role. A conversation is limited to 120 requests a minute (a deliverability or request read below counts as 5) and ends when the platform closes it (`closeConversation`).

The API reference lists *Workers Observability Write* for this endpoint: check a new token with one query before relying on it.

**Deliverability reads** (`src/support-deliverability.ts`, aggregates in `src/deliverability.ts`). Each returns `findings[]`: heuristics with a severity, a code, plain words and the numbers behind them.

**Redaction.** Never key values, hashes, webhook secrets, endpoint URLs (only their last four characters), app passwords or webhook payloads. A receiving server's reply to a bounce or a delay (`diagnosticCode`) is redacted wherever support reads it, in bounce reasons and in an email's events: the addresses and IP addresses in it are replaced by pattern (`<address>`, `<ip>`), at most 160 characters. Email bodies only in a chat whose person shared message content (the handoff's `shareContent`, or the content-access card), and only in that chat's workspace (`grant.contentWorkspaceId` in the context); an email conversation never reads content, even one bound to its sender. Every call is written to `audit_events` (actor: the conversation; 90 days), and each workspace's Activity shows "Nightmail support viewed this workspace" once per conversation, with whose request let support in.

**Support conversations** (`src/support-conversations.ts`). The support platform registers each conversation it holds (`registerConversation`, `POST /v1/operator/support/conversations`, the binding only) and Nightmail decides who it is for from evidence it checks itself:

- **Chat:** the signed handoff token. Nightmail checks its own signature and the unused handoff row, which opens one conversation, once (`409 SUPPORT_HANDOFF_USED` after that). It covers the handoff's workspace, with message content if the customer ticked it.
- **Email:** a message ID. Nightmail binds the conversation to an account only from evidence it stored itself, and only when all of these hold: the message was received at a support address (`NIGHTMAIL_SUPPORT_ADDRESS_IDS`; anything else is `404`); ingest's author check on that support address's link is set (`reply_author_verified`: every header name well formed, one literal From, consistent with the full parsed headers and with SES's common From, which must be present); SES's DMARC verdict is PASS (DKIM or SPF aligned with the From domain); the From as written (case kept, plain ASCII, checked before any lower-casing) is exactly one account's verified email (`email_verified`), the local part byte for byte and the domain in any case; a sign-in proved that address a mailbox its person reads within the last 30 days (`mailbox_proved_at`, recorded when a sign-in's proven mailbox is the account's address: hosted, an emailed link or code, or Google for a Google-hosted one; `account-policy.ts` `mailboxProven`, the one definition binding, the claim and the release use; an address only "verified once", by GitHub say, proves nothing); the message was written to support (a support address in its one To or Cc field, one Date within 3 days of receipt, one Message-ID, which binds that account once: `support_email_bindings`, so another delivery of the same email asks for a sign-in); and that account has a live workspace membership. Then `mode` is `member`, `verification` `dmarc`, and the registration is on the person's trail (`support.register`). A verified sender without such an account (or whose account has no workspace) is `non_member` and bound to nobody (general answers). Anything else is `pending_signin` (an account's address, up to case, that didn't bind) or `unverified`, bound to nobody, and its message is recorded for a sign-in (`support_email_signins`, migration 381). Each later message registers again, from the same sender only (`409 SUPPORT_CONVERSATION_SENDER`); an unbound conversation is bound by the first message that binds its sender, and a bound one takes only mail that binds the same account again (`409 SUPPORT_CONVERSATION_MISMATCH`): it is never rebound or downgraded. Binding counts against the person's 10 open email conversations, a cap of its own that never keeps them out of a chat (`429 SUPPORT_CONVERSATION_LIMIT`; the platform sends its fixed busy reply).
- **Sign-in (the claim):** first `GET /v1/support-conversations/{id}/claim?messageId=…` shows that person which email it is (its subject and when it arrived, never its content), with the same checks; then `POST /v1/support-conversations/{id}/claim { messageId }` from the person's own dashboard session, as accepting an invitation does (with or without a workspace; the dashboard's origin; the session's sign-in must prove the account's current verified address, so on a hosted installation an emailed link or code, or a Google sign-in for it). It succeeds only when that verified address is the message's sender (in any case) and a mailbox they proved within 30 days (`mailboxProven`), the message was recorded for a sign-in in that open conversation within the last 7 days, and no one else is bound to it or signed for it; anyone else gets `404`, a bearer credential `403`. Unconfirmed sign-ins (and the sender addresses they keep) are deleted after 7 days, Message-ID bindings after 7 days too (`maintenance.ts`). It binds an unbound conversation to them (`verification: signin`; `member` with a workspace, else `non_member`), records the sign-in for that one message only (also when the conversation is already theirs), writes an internal `support.claim` row once, and wakes the support platform with the conversation's ID only (again on a repeated claim). The scope lists signed-in messages (`conversation.signedIn`); the platform answers each one once. An address-only confirm link never binds anything.
- **Content access:** `POST /v1/support-conversations/{id}/content-access` lets the chat's own person share message content in its workspace. Email never can.

A person has at most 10 open chats and, apart from them, 10 open email conversations. `closeConversation` ends one. Reads go through `request(conversationId, method, path)`: the binding stamps the conversation on the runtime, never on the request, so no header, query parameter or body can name one. Over HTTP, `/v1/support/*` answers every credential with the same `403`. For each request, `supportGate` loads the open conversation, which must have a person. It joins that person's live memberships within the conversation's scope. It keeps only the workspaces where the person's role may call the route's dashboard counterpart (`SUPPORT_CEILINGS`: domains, webhooks, billing, the audit trail and request logs need an owner or admin, and a member reads received mail only on receiving addresses). The conversation has its own 120-a-minute window. Every call writes an internal `support.read` row. The first read of each workspace writes the customer-visible `support.view` row (`{ ticket, member, channel, verification, contentShared }`). `GET /v1/support/scope` names the person (`user.id`, for the platform's tickets and comparisons, never shown to the model; `user.mailboxProven`: whether their address is verified and a mailbox they proved within 30 days, which an answer written from their account requires; it goes to `user.email` as stored, though recipient case isn't preserved on the wire: the reply API lower-cases recipients), the workspace in scope and their role, an email conversation's signed-in messages, and `shrunk: true` once they left a workspace the conversation read. `POST /v1/support/scope { workspaceId }` narrows a bound email conversation to one of its person's workspaces, and answers for a chat's own workspace only.

**Support notes** (`src/support-notes.ts`). What support keeps about one person between their conversations. `POST /v1/support/notes { text }` (at most 1,000 characters) writes one for the conversation's person; the workspace it is tagged with is the conversation's scope at that moment (null when none is chosen), never a field of the request. A person keeps 50 live notes; another retires the oldest. `GET /v1/support/notes` returns that person's notes that are untagged or tagged with a workspace they still belong to, so a workspace's notes disappear once they leave it. Notes are never shown to other members. Staff read every live note of a person or conversation with `GET /v1/operator/support/notes?userId=…` or `?conversationId=…` (an operator's own dashboard session).

**The support inbox** (`src/support-inbox.ts`). Every conversation, its transcript and staff replies, for operators in the dashboard:

- **The support platform writes it** through the binding only. `mirrorMessages(conversationId, messages)` appends transcript entries in order: each keeps the support platform's own `msg_…` ID (sending one again changes nothing; an ID from another conversation is `409 SUPPORT_MESSAGE_CONFLICT`), the agent's tool calls by label and outcome only, and an agent answer's delivery (`sent`, or `held`: frozen before a staff reply and never sent). `mirrorState` sets the subject, the one ticket (`ticketed`), the turn and cost counters (they only grow), the count of blocked work items, and `pauseForTicket` (after an emailed ticket). It never resumes the agent. `inboxState` answers whether the agent is paused; check it before every turn and every send.
- **Operators read it** from their own dashboard session under `/v1/operator/support-inbox`, which is outside the binding's `/v1/operator/support/` prefix, so the support platform never reaches it; customers get `403`. `GET /v1/operator/support-inbox` lists conversations by latest activity (`view`: `needs_you`, `agent`, `tickets`, `needs_reply`, `paused`, `attention`; `channel`, `status`, `q` (which matches a conversation with no subject by the opening line it is listed under), cursor pages), with the sizes of Needs you and Agent handling (`counts`). `GET /v1/operator/support-inbox/{id}` returns the conversation page in one read: the transcript (its last 500 entries), the evidence that bound it, and the customer's panel: the account and its verification, every workspace with role, sending and standing, their other conversations and tickets (the same person's only; a conversation bound to nobody lists the others from its address that are bound to nobody), their latest audit entries and support's notes about them. Each read writes an internal `support.inbox_view` row.
- **Needs you** (`view=needs_you`, the dashboard's default) is what only a person can move forward: a conversation that nobody marked done since anything new happened (a message, a failed delivery, more blocked work, a ticket, a pause) and has an open ticket, blocked work, a latest answer or staff reply that failed to send, or a paused agent on an open conversation where staff owe the next message. It lists a P1 ticket first, then customers nobody else will answer (they wrote last and the agent is paused or blocked, or the last answer failed), then P2 tickets, then the rest, the longest waiting first within each (`needsYou.since`). **Agent handling** (`view=agent`) is every other open conversation whose agent is active.
- **Staff act with the `operator` role** (`operator:read` only reads): `POST …/{id}/replies { text }` with an `Idempotency-Key`, `…/pause`, `…/resume` (hand back to the agent), `…/resolve` (the ticket), `…/done` (out of Needs you, resolving an open ticket; send `{ ticketId }`, the ticket on screen or `null`, and one filed since answers `409 SUPPORT_TICKET_CHANGED`), `DELETE …/done` (Undo: the operator who marked it, within 10 minutes; it reopens the ticket that Done resolved) and `…/wake` ("Retry now"). Each change is one transaction with an internal `support.reply`, `support.pause`, `support.resume`, `support.resolve`, `support.done` or `support.undone` row. Read markers are each operator's own (`PUT …/{id}/read { read, seq? }`, `operator:read` included; `unread` in both reads) and never on the trail.
- **A staff reply** is stored `queued` and pauses the agent as that staff member. Nightmail then wakes the support platform: `POST <NIGHTMAIL_SUPPORT_URL origin>/hooks/nightmail-wake { conversationId }`, with no content, skipped without `NIGHTMAIL_SUPPORT_URL`. The support platform pulls the reply with `pullOutbox(conversationId?)`, from the wake or its own sweep, delivers it, and records `markDelivery(conversationId, messageId, 'sent' | 'failed')`. Staff can reply only while the outbox was pulled in the last 10 minutes (`replyAvailable` in both reads; otherwise `409 SUPPORT_REPLY_UNAVAILABLE`), so no reply waits for a support platform that is not running.
- **Retention:** transcripts and subjects are deleted 90 days after a conversation closes (hourly maintenance).

**Support actions** (`src/support-actions.ts`). A conversation can propose a change for its person, never make one: `POST /v1/support/actions` with a `kind` and the IDs its public route takes: `retry_webhook_delivery`, `test_webhook`, `retry_inbound_delivery`, `test_inbound_webhook`, `redeliver_message` or `pause_campaign`. Nightmail checks the target is in the conversation's scope and that the person's role may make the change there (all six need an owner or admin), writes the `summary` from its own records in plain words, without IDs (a webhook by its name and its URL's registrable domain, never the rest of the URL; a delivery by its event, time and outcome), as plain text, never to be rendered as Markdown or HTML: each name a person typed, such as a workspace, webhook or campaign name, is inserted JSON-quoted with its control and formatting characters removed, and `parts` gives the sentence's `template` and those `labels` apart), and keeps the proposal open 24 hours, at most 3 per conversation (proposing the same change again answers the open one). Only that person confirms it, signed in: `POST /v1/support-actions/{id}/confirm` from their own dashboard session (`GET /v1/support-actions/{id}` shows it, `…/decline` declines it); anyone else gets `404`, and any bearer credential or the binding `403`. Confirming re-checks their membership and role, then runs the public route's own function once, in the transaction that records the outcome: the result, or the route's refusal (`DELIVERY_PENDING`, `ENDPOINT_PAUSED`, `CAMPAIGN_NOT_PAUSABLE`, …) as `status: failed`. Confirming again answers the same. A test of an inbound webhook is checked and charged in that transaction and posted after it, as `running`; if its answer is never recorded within 5 minutes it settles as `failed` with `SUPPORT_ACTION_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, and it is never posted again. Each change writes its customer-visible audit entry (`webhook.test`, `webhook.delivery_retry`, `inbound_webhook.test`, `inbound_webhook.delivery_retry`, `inbound_message.redeliver`, or the campaign's own `campaign.pause`) with the person as the actor, and every decision and outcome an internal `support.action` row. The conversation reads the outcome with `GET /v1/support/actions/{id}`. `POST /v1/support/domains/{id}/verify` re-checks a domain's DNS as the dashboard's Verify does (an owner or admin's domain only), without a confirmation, and writes a customer-visible `domain.verify` entry (actor: the support conversation, for its person) and an internal `support.action` row.

**Server-side registration** (the support Worker, never the agent):

```ts
// wrangler: "services": [{ "binding": "BANE", "service": "nightmail-production", "entrypoint": "SupportPlatform" }]
const checked = await env.BANE.verifyHandoff(handoffToken);           // { ok, handoff: { id, userId, workspace, shareContent, usedAt, … } }
if (!checked.ok) return refuse(checked.code);
const registered = await env.BANE.registerConversation({ conversationId, evidence: { kind: 'handoff', token: handoffToken } });
// 201 → { mode: 'member', workspaceId, contentShared, … }. Every read (a chat's) names the conversation, never a person or a token:
const scope = await env.BANE.request(conversationId, 'GET', '/v1/support/scope');
const context = await env.BANE.request(conversationId, 'GET', `/v1/support/context?workspaceId=${scope.body.workspace.id}`);
```

The agent's tools are closures over that call in the support Worker (`support/src/agent/tools.ts`): no tool takes a person, conversation or token, so the model cannot widen what it reads.

## Billing modes

Hosted installations (`MULTI_TENANT=true`) bill a prepaid USD balance per workspace; self-hosted installations have no billing. Money is integer micro-cents (µ¢: 1¢ = 1,000,000 µ¢). Rate version 4: $0.20 per 1,000 emails sent (per recipient) or received (per message) and $0.25 per GB-month of received mail older than 90 days. AI decisions are free: still metered (meter `omen`) and settled, at $0. Each UTC month includes 5,000 sent and 20,000 received emails, counted separately (sends beyond 5,000 are charged even while received mail is still free, and the other way round), and 1 GB of that storage. A rate version prices whole UTC months, by the month usage was metered in (`billing_rates_in`): version 4 from November 2026, version 1 (3,000 sent and 3,000 received free, and AI decisions at $0.10 per 1,000) before it, so a late refund into an earlier month is priced as that month was. Versions 2 (5,000 and 5,000, decisions still charged) and 3 (5,000 and 5,000, decisions free) priced November 2026 until the next replaced them there; migration 380 gave back what November's decisions had been charged, and 476 what its 5,001st to 20,000th received messages had. IMAP/SMTP access is free, with a fair-use download limit of 5 GB a day and 30 GB a month per inbox (override per inbox with `inbox_state.egress_daily_bytes` / `egress_monthly_bytes`). BYO workspaces are metered at $0.

`billing_config.mode` is a database row, so switching needs no deploy and rolls back instantly:

| Mode | Meters | Settlement | 402 gate, campaign waits, notices, auto-recharge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `enforce` (the default since migration 300) | record | hourly; usage is taken from promotional credit, then paid credit | live |
| `shadow` (kill-switch) | record | hourly; usage rows post $0 with the would-be cost in `detail.costUc` | would-be denials log `BILLING_SHADOW_DENY`; notices are not sent |
| `off` (kill-switch) | record | none; usage metered while off is settled at $0 when billing comes back on | none |

Usage is priced under the mode it was metered in. `scripts/set-billing-enforcement.ts` settles every workspace under the old mode before it switches (except to `off`, so the kill-switch never waits on the settler), and a workspace's free flag starting or ending settles that workspace first.

- Metering adds no round trip: statement triggers on `sending_emails` (sends, pre-SES refunds into the current hour) and `mail_messages` (receives, `verdicts.omenCalls`, stored bytes per day). Sandbox mail (`region='sandbox'`, from `POST /v1/inboxes/{id}/simulate`) is never metered. Storage counts mail kept past 90 days: the raw size of `lifecycle='kept'` mail (default mail's raw MIME expires at 90 days; its extracted attachments aren't counted). Admission stays one statement; ingest stays two (three with files).
- The hourly job (`billing/cron.ts`) settles closed hours per workspace (`billing_settle`) and once a UTC day runs the storage day, the unpaid scan, the drift check (`BILLING_DRIFT`) and the BYO fair-use report. `BILLING_SETTLE_LAG` logs an error when unsettled usage is older than 3 hours.
- With an empty balance under `enforce`: the API returns `402 BALANCE_EXHAUSTED`, campaigns wait with `waiting_reason='balance'` until a credit, and SMTP submission gets `451 4.7.1`. AI decisions keep running: they are free. Received mail is accepted (and billed) for 7 days, then held under `held/` in the pool bucket when `billing_config.receive_hold` is on; a credit that leaves paid plus promotional credit at $0 or more releases it, and the hourly job releases any held mail of a workspace that is no longer held. A debt older than 60 days is written off, but the hold stays until a credit.
- `billing_accounts.mode`: `prepaid` (default) or `exempt` (settled at $0), derived from the free flags: a workspace is `exempt` while its own free flag or an owner's free account applies, and `prepaid` otherwise. There is no invoiced mode (migration 315 removed it; it refuses to run while any account is invoiced). `NIGHTMAIL_PLATFORM_DOMAIN_WORKSPACE` is never gated. Migration 249 made every workspace that existed at launch (all Anomaly's) `exempt`; workspaces created later are `prepaid`.
- Received mail that SES's spam scan flagged (verdict FAIL or GRAY, which ingest junks) is free: no receive unit, no free-tier use, no stored bytes. AI decisions on it are still metered, at $0 like any other.
- Prepaid credit expires 12 months after purchase. Each top-up is a credit lot (`billing_credit_lots`); paid usage, refunds and disputes spend the lot that expires first (promotional credit is still spent before paid credit), and refunds of usage go back to the credit spent last. The hourly settler sends one `credit_expiring` notice per top-up 30 days before its remaining credit expires, then, once the expiry hour has closed and the workspace is settled, posts the remainder as one `expire` ledger row (`expire:<workspace>:<lot>`). Unused credit is otherwise not refundable: operator refunds record a basis (a billing error, an account Anomaly closes, or a legal requirement).
- `billing_config.storage_billing_from` prices storage at $0 before that date (the 90 days' notice). `bill_simulated` meters installation-simulated sends (dev and E2E only).
- Operator money routes (credits, adjustments, write-off, refunds, close-refund) require an `Idempotency-Key`: a retry returns the first answer and moves no money twice.
- Turning on `receive_hold` needs `s3:PutObject`, `s3:GetObject` and `s3:DeleteObject` on `arn:aws:s3:::<pool bucket>/held/*` for the Worker's pool role, and no lifecycle rule on `held/`.

## Secret rotation and upgrades

A normal `bun run migrate` needs only `DATABASE_URL` and the intended `DEFAULT_SES_REGION` on first setup. Without rotation inputs it does not re-encrypt webhooks. If `NIGHTMAIL_ROOT_SECRET` is supplied, it first decrypts one stored webhook signing secret read-only; `KEY_ROTATION_REQUIRED` means the sample could not be decrypted and nothing was written. No stored webhook is required, and secretless CI skips this probe. Only explicit rotation requires the new root and previous key as described below.

`MIGRATION_LEDGER_AMBIGUOUS` means both `opensend_migrations` and `nightmail_migrations` exist. Stop and compare their migration names and checksums with the checkout. Only after confirming that `nightmail_migrations` is authoritative and that `opensend_migrations` is empty or contains only duplicate entries, drop the stray `opensend_migrations` and rerun. If they disagree, reconcile the histories first; do not replay migrations.

`NIGHTMAIL_ROOT_SECRET` is the root for authentication and HKDF-derived, domain-separated webhook encryption keys. Webhook ciphertext carries a version/key identifier. **Back up the old secret securely before rotation**; losing a needed decryption key can make stored signing secrets unrecoverable.

1. Generate a new root secret. Set the old root as `PREVIOUS_NIGHTMAIL_ROOT_SECRET` and the new root as `NIGHTMAIL_ROOT_SECRET` in every API/worker environment. Restart the services with the same configuration.
2. Run `bun run migrate` against the intended database (Docker: `docker compose -p bane-api-local run --rm api node dist/migrate.js`). Migration re-encrypts stored webhook secrets with the new root. Resolve any failure before retiring old key material.
3. Keep the previous root until migration is complete, all services use the new configuration, and on Cloudflare the feedback queues and their dead-letter queue hold no item tagged under the old root (items are tagged at enqueue and verified under either root at consumption). Then remove `PREVIOUS_NIGHTMAIL_ROOT_SECRET` and restart all services. Dashboard sessions are opaque database records and are revoked independently.

For an **older `ENCRYPTION_KEY` installation**, retain that old key only as upgrade-time decryption input while configuring a new `NIGHTMAIL_ROOT_SECRET`. Do **not** set both legacy `ENCRYPTION_KEY` and `PREVIOUS_NIGHTMAIL_ROOT_SECRET`. Run migration to re-encrypt secrets, verify completion, then remove `ENCRYPTION_KEY` and restart. Do not copy obsolete admin-key/bootstrap settings into a new deployment. Old credentials do not create a OIDC administrator.

**Stop before migration if the old installation used a custom `WORKSPACE_ID` or contains non-default namespace data.** The new installation uses a fixed default namespace, with no public workspace setting. It needs an explicit manual namespace plan covering database rows, queued work, and stored object keys; simply removing the old variable can make existing data inaccessible. Do not silently rename data or discard storage. Resolve any older in-progress encryption-key transition before this upgrade as well.

## Verification

The only acceptance-test file is `api/api.acceptance.test.ts`. Idempotency and access control are also model-checked by [`api/tear`](tear/README.md) against the same kind of isolated database. `bun run test` uses the local API/job worker and a paired local database; its OIDC identities are synthetic and its OAuth transport is mocked. It does **not** prove normal OIDC login with a real client. Do not run it against production or unrelated data: synthetic contacts/campaigns and audit records are created.

Use an isolated database with synthetic identities, never real user data, for local acceptance. For that dedicated local test installation, the suite needs the server's matching `NIGHTMAIL_ROOT_SECRET`, `API_BASE_URL`, and paired `DATABASE_URL`. Allow the fixture's subject `acceptance-operator` (`AUTH_TEST_SUBJECT`) in `AUTH_ALLOWED_SUBJECTS`. Reserve that subject and `operator@example.com` for the fixture; neither may collide with an existing user. Restart the API/worker after changing configuration. These are **test-only** identities/permissions, not production onboarding, and fake OAuth credentials alone are not an alternate login path.

The dashboard is desktop-first. At a 390-pixel viewport its controls remain reachable, but the persistent sidebar leaves a cramped main pane; small-screen layout polish remains a follow-up, not a verified mobile-ready claim.

Useful checks, from their respective packages:

- `api/`: `bun run check`, `bun run test`, `bun run sdk`, and `bun run cf:check` (after the dashboard build).
- `app/`: `bun run build`.

Live acceptance is opt-in with `LIVE_SES_TEST=1`, `SES_TEST_RECIPIENT`, and `SES_TEST_FROM` for explicitly authorized real sending. The commands above describe checks, not a claim that they have passed on your installation. Docker/Workers builds, local simulation, OIDC mocks, and source review do not replace the [external release gates](#ses-and-production-release-gates).

## Agent inboxes

`POST /v1/inboxes` creates an expiring inbox with a random address on one of the workspace’s active receiving hosts. It needs a workspace administrator or an API key that creates inboxes: `POST /v1/api-keys` with `access: {"type":"addresses","addressIds":[],"mail":["create","read"]}`, which then reads only the inboxes it created. The default lifetime and grace period are 24 hours each. A `clientId` replays a create without revealing its key again. Each workspace can create 60 inboxes per minute and keep 1,000 retained, counting grace and pending erasure. Keys that create inboxes and MCP must omit `localPart`; generated names cannot override an existing wildcard or auto-create catch-all. API keys for one inbox stop working when it is deleted. Reply sending is opt-in and limited to authenticated authors in the same inbox and thread. Like every address, an inbox has no hourly or daily send cap: the workspace’s sending limits apply, plus the loop guards (20 messages to one recipient an hour, 10 in one conversation per 10 minutes). `sendLimits.perHour` and `perDay` on addresses, `send.perDay` on inboxes and an app password’s `perDay` and `perMonth` are deprecated and not enforced; an address whose `perHour` or `perDay` is 0 still doesn’t send (`MAILBOX_SEND_DISABLED`), and an inbox without `send.enabled` still doesn’t reply (`EPHEMERAL_SEND_DISABLED`).

`GET /v1/inboxes/{id}/messages?wait=30` returns the oldest message after `after`, starting with the first delivery even if it arrived before the call; pass the returned `cursor` to advance in commit order. Junk, unverified and held mail never wake a waiter; list them with `include=held` (without `wait`). Mail past 200 messages or 25 MiB (or a lower configured cap), and mail during the grace period, is held: stored, readable and announced by `message.held`, never dropped. `inbox.created`, `inbox.deleted`, `message.held` and `inbox.quota_exceeded` are additive event types; consumers should ignore types they don’t know. The hourly sweep erases due inboxes and their unreferenced messages and objects, and releases real addresses to normal domain routing. An admin can release an old reservation with `DELETE /v1/inbound/retired/{address}`. Erasure does not shorten database backup retention or remove copies another address still holds.

Without a receiving domain, create `type: "sandbox"` and POST `{ from, subject, text, html? }` to `/v1/inboxes/{id}/simulate`. Its generated `.invalid` host is permanently disabled for external mail and has no SES resources. Simulated mail uses the same store and cursor as received mail, is marked `simulated: true` and has no sender authentication. The limits are 50 per inbox and 1,000 per workspace per UTC day; sandbox inboxes never send.

**Storage.** On hosted Nightmail, agent inboxes are available once `NIGHTMAIL_MAIL_STORE=r2` (see the [rollout steps](../docs/receiving-operations.md#mail-store-rollout-and-rollback)), and shared-pool inbox mail always goes to R2, so erasing it needs no S3 delete grant. Mail kept in S3 (self-hosted installs and BYO connections) is erased only if the storage role has `s3:DeleteObject` on `workspaces/*/raw/*` and `workspaces/*/att/*`; the BYO template does not grant it. A denied delete leaves a failed `mail.deleteObjects` job holding its keys, and raw MIME still expires through the bucket’s 90-day rule.

For upgrades, supply the former `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET` value as `NIGHTMAIL_ROOT_SECRET` (or `BANE_ROOT_SECRET`) to preserve encrypted webhooks and agent-token signatures. Hosted CI reads the existing AWS secret under its historical name.
